THE MORE JESUS = MORE REALITY EXPERIMENT

The Investigator Was Under Construction

The One-Week Menu, Norm Wood, Jesus Groundhog Day, and the many ways God may wrought upon His children.

By Saint Greg — Wake-Up-with-Jesus reflections, November 4, 2026

What if the things I was drawn to investigate were not merely the subjects of my education, but also the instruments of it?

1. The Investigator Was Under Construction

I woke this morning in my Wake-Up-with-Jesus scene with an unexpected convergence of old puzzle pieces. I understand the experience as Jesus inviting me to see a pattern in the way I have been drawn, over decades, to investigate the ordinary and search for its hidden first principles.

What if the things I have been drawn to investigate throughout my life were not merely the subjects of my education, but also the instruments of it? What if, while I investigated the One-Week Menu, God was also developing the investigator who would someday turn those same capacities toward investigating Him?

2. Jesus Groundhog Day: The Wheels Go Round and Round

I geeked out for a decade-plus over something that sounds almost comically domestic: the recurring meals of human existence. The wheels go round and round. People eat breakfast, lunch, and dinner, then do it again. They say they could never eat the same things every week, while often doing approximately that already.

There it was: an underlying One-Week Menu approximation, hiding in plain sight. I did not invent recurrence. I recognized it. I named it. I began asking how to make it better. Survey the many. Choose the few. Inhabit the one.

3. The Easy Discovery and the Hard Tradeoffs

The conceptual breakthrough was almost embarrassingly simple. But optimizing the system was a lifetime-sized problem. Taste, ease of preparation, cost, nutritional density, correlation of ingredients, shopping, storage, cleanup, and the preferences of other people in my home all tugged in different directions.

The menu was not merely a list of recipes. It was an investigation into approximations, competing goods, and what happens when a person deliberately improves a recurring system. A family could inherit the principles without copying every meal. That was the OneHome ambition: all we have, why we have it, how to copy and customize it, and how to teach others.

4. Norm Wood and the Gifts of Human Difference

Then there were the years with Dr. Norm Wood. Norm could geek out on human functioning as relentlessly as I could geek out on a menu. Personality, gifts, weaknesses, measurement, dispositions, practices, the expanding theory of self: we spent years exploring that territory.

I now wonder whether those investigations were another part of the preparation. Norm helped me look for underlying patterns in human beings, just as the menu taught me to look for patterns in meals. Perhaps God was honing an instrument through repeated use, even before I understood what I would eventually use it to investigate.

5. 2015: Another Story Must Begin

In 2015, my investigative appetite turned with increasing intensity toward the Story of Jesus. Who is this Jesus? How does a personal relationship with an invisible Redeemer work? Is there an underlying structure, a recurring approximation, a spiritual menu hiding in plain sight?

The subject changed. The machinery of inquiry did not. I wanted to get MORE Jesus, and I wanted to get to the heart of the matter. Where is the E = mc²? I know you are hiding there! And this morning the pieces seemed to fit: perhaps I was being drawn all along. Drawn. Wrought upon. Drawn again.

6. Is the Desire Itself a Gift?

Here is the important distinction. Perhaps God did not merely find a convenient later use for a personality I happened to possess. Perhaps the desire to investigate—the appetite, the persistence, the delight in first principles—was itself among the ways He wrought upon me.

I cannot demonstrate that this is what God intended. I can say that, as I pondered this morning and tried to #HearHim, the interpretation illuminated decades of my life. The investigator himself may have been under construction while he thought he was constructing menus and theories.

7. Moroni 10, Myers-Briggs, and the Many Gifts of God

And if this is a possible way God works upon me, why would I assume He works upon everybody through the same psychological doorway? Moroni 10 and 1 Corinthians 12 speak of diverse gifts. What if our theology of gifts has been too narrow?

One person is drawn to coherence, another to harmony, another to music, comedy, invention, service, craftsmanship, storytelling, or beauty. Personality frameworks such as Myers-Briggs offer one vocabulary for considering differences, although fixed MBTI types are not established scientific categories. The broader reality of differing temperaments, interests, and abilities is unmistakable.

Perhaps God works with and through those differences. Perhaps He even guides development through circumstances and experience. Questions about DNA, prenatal life, and childhood environments are more speculative, and they bring difficult questions about suffering and freedom. I do not need to settle them to begin exploring the possibility.

8. Axl Rose and the Unwitting Builders of Zion

What about Axl Rose? What about the millions of other Axl Roses, people whose remarkable gifts do not arrive wrapped in conventional religious language? What if some are being wrought upon, and contributing something to the eventual building of Zion, wittingly or unwittingly?

That does not mean every expression of a gift is good, or that every impulse is divine. A gift can be real without every use of it being righteous. But it may mean that the body of Christ is larger, stranger, and more various in its preparatory work than our usual categories allow.

Perhaps people are drawn toward different parts of the elephant. And perhaps they are also given different ways of touching the same part. A musician and a mathematician may both investigate love, but through different instruments. No single person's way of knowing is enough.

9. Everybody Already Has a Spiritual Groundhog Day

The menu taught me to notice recurrence. Now look at human attention. People wake, desire, remember, practice, repeat, and become increasingly shaped by what they attend to and love. This happens whether or not they recognize the process as spiritual.

Everybody already has some approximation of a recurring spiritual life: repeated attention, affection, habits, and formation. Not necessarily Christian. Not necessarily conscious. But real. Can these existing capacities be redirected toward remembering and loving God? That is Jesus Groundhog Day. That is the MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment.

10. The First Commandment: There You Are!

Then I come upon the first and great commandment: love God with all your heart, soul, and mind (Matthew 22:37–38). Of course! The prime directive. Get MORE love of God than I had before. All you need is love, as the Beatles sang.

But here comes the One-Week Menu lesson again. Naming the fundamental principle is the easy part. Living its tradeoffs is hard. God is FRICKING INVISIBLE. He can be hard to hear. He barely seems to save sometimes. He appears to do head fakes. My experience of His presence is not always clear, predictable, or comfortable.

I can ask why I struggle to love Him more without pretending the difficulty has vanished. The first commandment is simple to state and inexhaustible to inhabit. Perhaps this is exactly the kind of problem I have been trained, by a thousand apparently ordinary investigations, to keep exploring.

11. The Sacred Clown Was Being Trained, Too

Wait a minute. I have been calling myself an investigator, a first-principles fellow, a pattern recognizer. All true enough. But I have left out something essential about my way of knowing: mashUP. Sacred Clown moves.

I do not merely discover patterns within established categories. I press apparently unrelated things together and see what happens. I take Adrian Monk's peculiar habits and explore vicarious service. I take Groundhog Day and turn it into a laboratory for recurring encounters with Jesus. I take a family menu and ask what it can teach me about the First Commandment. Rock songs, nano-marathons, pillows, television, gum wrappers, and the ordinary stuff of life become possible instruments of remembrance.

This is JESUS (MUDD)LER territory: deliberately pressing, twisting, combining, and reimagining ordinary ingredients in hopes of releasing extraordinary, Christ-reminding possibilities. Jesus does not always give me new ingredients. Perhaps He teaches me to taste the ones I have already been given.

The Sacred Clown makes the improbable move: What if these two things, which everyone knows do not belong together, are somehow speaking about the same underlying reality? The investigator then asks whether the collision reveals an actual principle or merely an amusing resemblance. The systems thinker asks whether it generalizes. And the Jesus-Greg experiment asks whether any of it helps me remember and love Jesus more.

The clown makes the strange move. The investigator searches for the underlying principle. The JESUS (MUDD)LER turns the discovery into something I can practice.

Maybe the willingness to look ridiculous is not incidental to this gift. Maybe it is part of what makes the gift possible. A person determined always to look sensible will avoid some of the very experiments that make my Jesus-Greg world possible.

And this widens the gifts question again. Some people uncover truths within categories. Some cross the boundaries between them. Some translate discoveries into experiences others can inhabit. No one way of knowing is sufficient. Perhaps God is working upon different people through all of these ways, wittingly or unwittingly.

So the investigator was not the only one under construction. The Sacred Clown was being trained, too.

12. The Gift May Be the Investigation

Suppose fifteen years of menu investigation produced only three useful principles. Would those years be wasted? No. I was also learning to recognize hidden recurrence, tolerate prolonged curiosity, weigh competing goods, and translate an implicit pattern into a teachable system.

Those capacities came with me when I turned toward Jesus. The menu may have improved family life. Norm's investigations may have clarified gifts and weaknesses. But perhaps both also prepared a particular kind of investigator for a particular kind of Jesus work.

And this possibility calls for humility. Other people are drawn toward realities I overlook. Other gifts may disclose dimensions of God's love that my first-principles approach cannot reach by itself. The body needs all its parts.

13. The Question That Will Keep Me Busy

What if God is doing something analogous with everyone—working through distinctive gifts, dispositions, desires, and experiences, often without their recognizing Him, preparing each to contribute something the rest of the body needs?

What if I begin not merely by asking how to love God more, but by noticing how I have already been taught to love, attend, investigate, and desire—and asking how those capacities can be offered back to Him?

This morning I see the One-Week Menu with Jesus mud on my eyes. I see Norm. I see 2015. I see the First Commandment. I see the wheels spinning round and round. Another story must begin. And perhaps, in an important sense, it already began a long time ago.
