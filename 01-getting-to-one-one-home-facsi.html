Family and Community Support Institute (FACSI) · Working concept

Getting to One — One Home

A tighter ship, with sufficiency, order, and simplicity. A family-centered framework for bringing what a household has, wants, and needs into better alignment—one practical realm at a time.

Have · Want · Need

Families assess what they currently have, what they actually want, and what they genuinely need. They identify tensions and make decisions: keep, improve, acquire, use, share, or release. The goal is not rigid uniformity, but a coherent, sustainable household system that lightens burdens.

Start with one realm—or the whole home

Food, home possessions and maintenance, health care, employment, money, family responsibilities, and life administration can each be addressed individually. Over time these realms can be connected into a whole-home plan.

Food: the living prototype

Jesus Mountain Restaurant documents one family's evolving recurring “One-Week Menu.” The symbolic introductory special, “The Three Men I Admire Most,” remains distinct from the weekly rotation. General Tso's chicken is the first working recurring meal: prepared in monthly freezer batches and served weekly, with a future Jesus-related name.

Each recurring menu item will eventually connect to a family-approved recipe, batch preparation, nutrition and cost evaluation, spiritual meaning and rituals, pantry and freezer inventory, shopping, food preservation, and garden planning. The menu drives supplies rather than unused supplies driving the menu.

From family experiment to community service

FACSI may eventually build an accessible family-assessment app, drawing on the AI-assisted Utah Medicaid guide as an early service model. Families could choose only the food module or work toward the larger Getting to One framework. The practical purpose is to help families bear one another's burdens—beginning with the developer's own family and children.

Concept document; not a launched FACSI application.
