II. WALK & TALK (20 minutes - open discussions as we walk around Dave's block)

Greg:

WHO is Greg, what energizes him?
I'm looking at your descriptions and dialog below from the "balcony in my mind" and recalled a file I created for you in 18-Jan-2007. I saw you then, and still see you, as a combination of an ENTP and ENFP. I've attached a PDF of that comparison for you (ignore the print date of today in the lower left.)

On the first page, a few lines down, you can see the "conflict styles" of ENTPs and ENFPs. My sense is that you must create an "Hegelian Dialectic" in your mind, then with others, about important ideas and issues in order to find meaning and resolution. Here is a link to a definition.

http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialectic

Read the four "metaphysical concepts" and then check to see if Heraclitus' style is also yours, "...all is in constant change, as a result of inner strife and opposition."

Opposites and opposition energize you, while others are mortified by conflict, and avoid it like the plague -- both with conflicting ideas and people.

But, your stamina for the pursuit of the dialectic would make you a great negotiator. How about let's get you out of town more often, to go help with the Palestinian-Israeli conflict, or some other issue, writ large, of your choice! I'm not kidding! Senator George Mitchell (special envoy) could use your energy because few have the stamina to stand in the gap created by opposites as you have!

TRA and the Senate Banking Committee
I went back and forth this afternoon listening to the re-run on C-SPAN of Thursday's hearing by the Senate Banking Committee. Much was said by the senators and the witnesses about the ineffectiveness and misuse of the first $350B released to the financial industry in the "bailout" last October. These two statements reflect the flavor of the hearing.

1. Prof. Elizabeth Warren (Harvard prof. and chairman of the oversight committee) was queried by Senator Bennett of Utah, about what to do about the mess created by the first half of the TARP bailout She said, "The time for 'Trust me' is over ... I'm an empiricist looking for the data ... then we will know what to do." (btw, she was in Zone 4 all the way in her interactions.) The senate committee is looking to her and her oversight committee for specifics on how to proceed. They are going to follow the data, if they can find it! The power of data during crunch time emerges.

2. Senator Evan Bayh of Indiana said, "Not one dime of the additional $350B will be released unless three conditions are met: accountability, accountability, and accountability."

So, while the Treasury Dept., the Fed, and the bankers may say, "We already do it..." (we are measuring where the money goes), nobody in the committee believes that they are doing it well enough to restore confidence in the banking system, and neither do I as a citizen.

I wish I had a graduate student looking for a project, and I'd have him/her listen to the hearing again -- with a clicker/counter -- to see how many dozens of times the words "Transparent ... Responsible ... Accountable" were used. The time has come for us all to do TRA better than we are doing it now. The debate has long been over in my mind, and I'm glad that it seems to be over now in congress!

Back to mapping and measuring so I can be more TRA...

--Norm
