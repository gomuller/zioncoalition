JESUS MOUNTAIN RESTAURANT · TEACHER'S WORKING KIT

Six Lessons: Learning to See the Restaurant Jesus Has Been Growing

Heavy Jesus saturation. Practical exercises. Printable worksheets. No scheduled class or enrollment is implied.

Governing question: What if you've already been running a little Zion restaurant at home, and Jesus is giving you eyes to see it?

How to use this kit

These are prototype 45–60 minute sessions, not an established course. Suggested rhythm: 5 minutes of welcome and prayer or reflection (participant's choice), 10 minutes of story and scripture, 20–30 minutes of hands-on practice, 10 minutes completing the worksheet, 5 minutes naming a witting moment. A participant can take one session without committing to six.

Facilitator principle: Jesus is not a decorative question added to household instruction. In Greg's Jesus-Greg WORLD, Jesus is the reason to reinterpret ordinary feeding as sacred service, the reason to discover an inheritance already present, and the imagined companion in developing a small prototype of Zion. Never tell another person what Jesus has privately revealed to them; invite them to explore and describe their own experience.

Original roots: the historical Food Kit envisioned an A-to-Z map of planning, gathering and storing, routines, and food story. The lesson sequence here is a new teaching prototype, not a verbatim transcription. Inheritance #1 and the larger story.

SESSION 1 · PROTOTYPE 45–60 MINUTES

Your World, Your Kits

Scripture: Matthew 25:38–40

Opening Jesus question: Jesus, have I been serving at Your restaurant without recognizing it?

Facilitator's story / teaching script

Invite learners to remember one meal they prepared or received. Read Matthew 25. Say: In this Jesus-Greg WORLD experiment, Jesus is not added to the meal afterward. He is the reason we are looking again at whom we fed, who carried burdens, and which gifts were already in the kitchen. Becoming witting means recognizing an inheritance already in use.

Do it together

  1. Tell the story of one ordinary family meal; name who was fed and what burden was lifted.
  2. Draw MY WORLD in the center. Around it, draw Food, Home, Health, Relationships, Work/Service, and other practical kits.
  3. Connect Food to two other kits: for example, shared meals to relationships, or pantry inventory to finances.
  4. Circle one existing skill or blessing that might be preparation for something larger.

Leave with: World → Kits → Food Kit map.

SESSION 2 · PROTOTYPE 45–60 MINUTES

The Power of One — SOS

Scripture: Alma 37:6

Opening Jesus question: Jesus, what small and simple thing have You been teaching me all along?

Facilitator's story / teaching script

Explain SOS: Sufficiency (enough), Order (a sequence that works), Simplicity (fewer unnecessary decisions). Imagine Jesus not merely blessing efficiency but making room for care, rest, welcome, and attention. A repeatable pancake routine can become a little act of service rather than a recurring crisis.

Do it together

  1. Choose one meal the household already makes.
  2. Write the minimum ingredients, tools, portions, and time needed for sufficiency.
  3. Arrange the steps in a dependable order; identify any advance preparation.
  4. Remove one needless decision or task, preserving joy and personal choice.
  5. Name one way the freed attention can be given to another person.

Leave with: One Meal / One Routine card.

SESSION 3 · PROTOTYPE 45–60 MINUTES

Your One Week Menu

Scripture: Matthew 25:35

Opening Jesus question: Jesus, whom are we really feeding when we make this menu?

Facilitator's story / teaching script

Read the hungry-and-fed passage. Explain that Jesus is encountered in the people being fed, not only in an opening prayer. The menu is a family agreement, not the cook's private decree. Dietary differences, energy, money, taste, and hospitality matter because people matter.

Do it together

  1. Ask household members for favorite meals, restrictions, and disliked foods.
  2. Choose seven realistic dinner anchors, allowing repeat meals and substitutions.
  3. Identify shared ingredients to reduce expense and waste.
  4. Ask whose labor is assumed; assign preparation and cleanup fairly.
  5. Invite common consent and schedule a short review after the first week.

Leave with: Seven-Day Jesus Mountain menu.

SESSION 4 · PROTOTYPE 45–60 MINUTES

Assemble the Food Kit

Scripture: Mosiah 18:8–9

Opening Jesus question: Jesus, can a pantry shelf become preparation to bear another's burden?

Facilitator's story / teaching script

A Food Kit is more than a recipe collection. It is organized capacity to feed people. Revisit the older Food Kit sequence: Plan → Gather/Store → One-time setup and routines → Food Story. Treat this as redemption of earlier work, not invention from nothing.

Do it together

  1. Select one meal from the weekly menu and write a recipe with quantities.
  2. Inventory pantry, freezer, refrigerator, tools, and containers.
  3. Separate what is available from what must be purchased or shared.
  4. Write one advance-preparation action and its safe storage instructions.
  5. File the recipe, shopping list, storage location, and serving story together.

Leave with: Food Kit starter packet.

SESSION 5 · PROTOTYPE 45–60 MINUTES

Run Your Home Restaurant

Scripture: Matthew 25:40

Opening Jesus question: Jesus, what will Your restaurant look like at our table tonight?

Facilitator's story / teaching script

In sacred imagination, invite Jesus to the table and make room for the unseen multitude; in visible practice, pay attention to the actual people present. A prototype of Zion is not measured by theatrical perfection. It is measured by food, welcome, burdens shared, and the willingness to learn.

Do it together

  1. Prepare and serve one menu meal using only the written Food Kit instructions.
  2. Invite another person to follow the instructions and note unclear steps.
  3. Record approximate cost, active time, portions, and cleanup effort.
  4. Ask diners what nourished them and what could improve.
  5. Revise the kit and identify one moment of witting—seeing Jesus in old work.

Leave with: Service and improvement log.

SESSION 6 · PROTOTYPE 45–60 MINUTES

Helper Friend — Share the Harvest

Scripture: Alma 37:6; Matthew 25:40

Opening Jesus question: Jesus, can I give this away without needing to be the guru?

Facilitator's story / teaching script

Imagine Jesus inviting you to teach one person, without demanding that you own a movement. The Helper Friend listens and accompanies; the learner retains agency. The AI can remain quiet until invited. Some learners may want explicitly Jesus-centered language; others may benefit from a practical, less religious translation. Sharing the method does not diminish the gift.

Do it together

  1. Invite one friend to describe their own food-system goal in their own words.
  2. Ask what is already working before suggesting changes.
  3. Demonstrate one useful tool only with permission; let the friend practice it.
  4. Offer recipes and methods freely, including mistakes and limitations.
  5. Agree on one follow-up; let the friend choose whether to continue.

Leave with: One-page Helper Friend handoff.

Facilitator's final reflection

Things as they were: the old meals and practices. Things as they are: the gift of recognition and order. Things as they are to come: a modest glimpse of Zion enacted through food and fellowship. The teacher's goal is not to produce a perfect system but to help someone become more witting, more capable, and more generous.

Working draft for review. Food-safety practices and nutrition requirements should be checked against current authoritative guidance when implementing specific menus or storage routines. The lessons are spiritual interpretations and practical experiments, not guarantees about divine intention or future events.
