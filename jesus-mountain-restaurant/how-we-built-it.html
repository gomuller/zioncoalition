A living documentary · Building Zion in the open

How Jesus and Greg Built Jesus Mountain Restaurant Together—with the Angels

A restaurant, a home, a work of performance art, and an ongoing account of what I believe Jesus and I are building together.

Imagine watching Noah build the ark. One morning there is an idea, another morning some lumber, and then a little more of an ark. People passing by can see the work. That is how I want to build Jesus Mountain Restaurant: in the open. You can watch the idea become a meal, a sign, a bag, a website, a delivery, a gathering—and perhaps something neither of us has imagined yet.

This is a documentary. In time there may be photographs, movies, scenes, conversations, and dated updates. For now, here is the beginning of the story, as I remember and understand it.

One of the forms of prayer is pondering.Even when we do not recognize it as prayer.

Before there was a restaurant

Years ago I found myself pondering, with a heart drawn toward people who had less than I did: the poor, the needy, people without homes. I wanted to care for them as Jesus did. I was imagining the familiar, more formal ways of helping.

Then came an understanding I believe Jesus was giving me: Greg, caring for your own children belongs in that same story. I had brought children into the world—people who began utterly dependent on being housed, fed, loved, and cared for. If all I ever did were spend my days caring for my family, that would be holy work. Adam and Eve knew something about that.

I had been looking outward for a calling that was already happening at my kitchen table. This did not lessen my concern for literally homeless neighbors; it enlarged the meaning of bearing one another’s burdens. My family needs food. Friends need food. I need the food—physical and spiritual—that other people bring to me.

The story that gives the work strength

A burden can feel different when I know what story I am living. I believe Jesus was helping me see ordinary cooking, shopping, cleaning, and feeding as part of His story. A pot of food is still a pot of food. But now it can also be a scene in the work of Zion.

After my born-again experience in 2015, I painted signs that said Jesus People Potluck and invited people to come. Mostly friends came, sometimes their friends, whoever wanted to be there. I now think of every meal I serve as a Jesus People Potluck—even when I provide all the food. People bring friendship, stories, songs, love, and spiritual nourishment. Looking back, I am learning to recognize Jesus in meals and memories where I did not recognize Him before.

The restaurateur in me

I have long been the sort of person who goes into a restaurant and starts imagining another restaurant. Wouldn't Sanpete County benefit from a Thai restaurant? What would I do differently? What is working beautifully here? My inventive, entrepreneurial ENTP mind has been rehearsing restaurant ownership for years.

In my understanding, Jesus has been able to work with that part of me, too. The dream began coming out of my mouth as Jesus Restaurant. Later I joined it to the mountain southeast of our home: Jesus Mountain Restaurant. The name made the dream more particular, more local, more real.

What we're building, one ordinary act at a time

First: the pondering. The desire to feed people, and the realization that caring for my own family is already part of the mission.

Then: the practice. Decades of home cooking, Jesus People Potlucks, meals shared with friends, and food carried to people who need it.

Then: the name. Jesus Restaurant becomes Jesus Mountain Restaurant, beneath the mountain I call Jesus Mountain.

Now: the visible signs. A website, a logo, stencils, brown paper bags, and a growing identity. Recently my friend Brad VanDyke asked whether Jesus Restaurant could make him a couple of sandwiches. Of course it could. I made them and delivered them. Soon, I hope, those sandwiches can arrive in a bag bearing the restaurant's own mark. Call it Jesus Uber Eats, if you like.

And the ordinary kitchen work continues. Even precooking chicken for General Tso's later in the week is a scene worth remembering. The restaurant is built in those small preparations, not only in grand announcements.

Who is “we”?

When I say we, I mean Jesus and me—and the people He may bring into the work. I also believe the Holy Ghost and angels are involved in ways I cannot fully see. I do not present my guesses about unseen participation as camera-recorded facts. This documentary will distinguish what I did, what others did, what I believe, and what I imagine Jesus may be inviting me to see.

One of the foundational messages I want this restaurant to reinforce is that we bear one another's burdens. Another is that those who are with us are more than we previously supposed. The restaurant gives those beliefs an address in my imagination, a place at the table, and something to do on a Tuesday afternoon.

A real project, a real art piece

It is a hobby. It is art. It is work. It is a restaurant approximation centered on hospitality at home. I am not charging money for the meals. My greatest compensation is the possibility of spending time with Jesus while I do the work—and learning to believe more deeply that I am not working alone.

I expect to invite people into my home, including people who are homeless. I have already fed people, both at home and beyond it. The documentary can show how the invitation grows, what succeeds, what changes, and what Jesus may teach me next.

The documentary continues

This page is the first entry, not the finished story. Future entries can include dated progress notes, pictures of the bags and stencils, actual meals and deliveries, video scenes, conversations with guests who wish to participate, and reflections on what we learned.

Today’s chapter: the restaurant has a name, a website, a developing visual identity, and a long history of real meals. The next chapter is being made in the kitchen.

We start rough. We build a little more. We look again. We remember who may have been with us all along.
