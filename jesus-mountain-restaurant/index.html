Our Family's Weekly Table

A new kind of restaurant: a living menu, revisited together every week.

Rather than pretending the menu was carved in stone, we ask again each week: Is this still what we want to cook, share, and eat? Here is the family's seven-day rhythm. Explore alternatives, follow recipes, and imagine your own family table.

Common consent, practiced at the family table. Inspired in part by Andy Groft’s explorations of Zion, our future Family Table Council will invite every family member to contribute—not merely to cast a vote, but to speak, listen, reconsider, and work toward a decision everyone can willingly support. Voting can reveal preferences; it is not a substitute for patient discussion or consensus. No one should be intimidated, silenced, or pressured into agreement. The question is not only “What won?” but “Have we heard one another, and what can we agree to try together?”

Our Story

A family restaurant, a living experiment, and one hoped-for inheritance.

Jesus Mountain Restaurant is centered on a home kitchen in Spring City, Utah, beneath Horseshoe Mountain — the mountain Greg calls Jesus Mountain. It grows out of decades of family meals, hospitality, and memory, and it is part of My Jesus-Greg WORLD: an invitation to reconsider ordinary meals, kitchens, and family gatherings as potentially extraordinary experiences — because feeding people can be a sacred act.

Greg sees this restaurant as one of one hundred late-life inheritances he hopes Jesus will help him receive. Read the longer reflection on inheritance, belief, and the Nanomarathon.

The Fine Print of Hospitality

Jesus Mountain Restaurant is performance art—and 100% real. It is also a home gathering and, in our estimation, always a Jesus People Potluck. Families feed families; friends feed friends. No money is charged. Guests need not bring food: conversation, friendship, testimony, song, and the spiritual nourishment we give one another are contributions of infinite worth.

Every meal here, past and present, belongs to the Jesus People Potluck—even when we didn't recognize it. This description expresses our hospitality, not an exemption from applicable law.
