Patio Classroom · first real learner · pilot protocol

Come to the table

A practical, Jesus-saturated guide for trying Jesus, My World, My Kits with one willing person.

Status: ready to invite · no real learner session recorded yet.

What are we testing?

Not whether a learner agrees with Greg. Not whether they use all the Jesus language. We want to know whether they can see an ordinary practice with new gratitude, organize it into a useful Food Kit, and choose a small act of service they can actually do.

“Lord, when saw we thee an hungred, and fed thee?” — Matthew 25:37 (KJV)

Greg's own faith-filled hypothesis is that Jesus may have been preparing the restaurant through decades of family meals. We tell that story generously, while leaving learners free to name their own experiences and beliefs.

1 · Invite one person

Choose someone who can comfortably say no, and who is willing to spend about 45–60 minutes exploring an ordinary household practice. They need not be an expert cook or agree with every interpretation.

Suggested invitation:

“I've been building a little teaching experiment called Jesus Mountain Restaurant. It starts with an idea I love: perhaps Jesus has been teaching us through the ordinary ways we've fed and cared for people. Would you be willing to try one lesson with me? It takes about an hour. I'd appreciate candid feedback about what is clear, confusing, or useful. There's no test of your beliefs, and you can stop whenever you like.”

This is an invitation script, not a message sent to anyone.

2 · Prepare the table

☐ Open or print Lesson #1 and its worksheet
☐ Open the Inheritance Map as a reference, not a requirement
☐ Bring a pen and blank paper
☐ Ask permission before writing down any quotations
☐ Agree that no names or personal stories will be published without separate permission
☐ Decide whether the learner wants a prayer, quiet reflection, or simply to begin

3 · Run the 55-minute session

Minutes 0–5 · Welcome

Explain that this is a prototype. Ask for an ordinary meal they remember. If the learner wants, open with prayer; otherwise begin with reflection.

Minutes 5–15 · Jesus and the meal

Read or summarize Matthew 25:35–40. Share Greg's pancake example. Ask what acts of care were already present. Do not insist that the learner reach the same conclusion.

Minutes 15–30 · Draw their world

Define a kit in everyday language: an organized set of tools, information, and habits for one part of life. Ask them to draw My World, name at least three kits, and connect two of them. If stuck, offer the fictional Tuesday-pasta example in Lesson #1.

Minutes 30–45 · Follow one meal

Use the original Food Kit sequence: Plan → Gather/Store → Setup/Routine → Food Story. Ask for one detail under each heading. Let the learner choose their own meal.

Minutes 45–55 · Witting and one action

Ask what the learner now notices that they hadn't named before. They may call it gratitude, witting, an insight, or nothing yet. Ask them to choose one doable action before next week. Then ask what was confusing.

5 · What counts as progress?

A good pilot can reveal that the lesson is too long, that the word “kit” needs more explanation, or that the sacred framing is powerful for one learner but difficult for another. Those are useful findings, not failures. The real measure is whether a person leaves with a clearer understanding and a practical way to love or serve someone.

After the session: summarize only observed findings, revise Lesson #1, and add a dated documentary note. Do not publish participant details without permission. If no session has happened, do not mark this milestone complete.
