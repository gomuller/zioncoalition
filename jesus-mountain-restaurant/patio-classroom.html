THE PATIO CLASSROOM · WORKING CURRICULUM

Come to dinner. Come back to learn with Jesus.

You're not coming alone. Pull up a chair. Make room for the angels. We are building this classroom on the conviction that Jesus has been at the table all along—and that there are more with us than we previously supposed.

You like our fruits? Come learn to grow your own tree—with Jesus.

A restaurant that gives away its secrets

Enjoy our food? We will show you the recipe, the technique, the shopping, the preparation, and the compromises we made. We'll help you make it healthier, cheaper, or better suited to your home. We may teach you to grow the herbs—or introduce you to a neighbor who grows them and might trade for your eggs. Our purpose is not to keep you dependent on our kitchen. It is to help you flourish in yours, with Jesus.

We imagine Jesus teaching through the cook, the child with an unexpected question, the gardener, the neighbor, and the friend who accompanies you. Every meal can become an entrance into His larger work.

The Power of One: why a One Week Menu?

We are practicing Getting to One: sufficiency, order, simplicity, and a life increasingly gathered around Jesus. One carefully developed meal can teach dozens of skills. A small, repeatable set of meals becomes a dependable weekly rhythm, while leaving room for discovery, celebration, and variety. One thing becomes many. By small and simple things are great things brought to pass (Alma 37:6).

Six sessions: Jesus helps us recognize and grow our Food Kit

We do not begin by asking Jesus to bless an otherwise secular course. We begin with the possibility that He has been preparing this kitchen, these people, these habits, and these gifts for years. Every exercise is a chance to become more witting about that preparation. Matthew 25 is not an optional devotional at the start; it changes what we believe serving dinner means.

1. Your World, Your Kits

Begin with the end in mind. Imagine your own world, with Jesus at its center and practical kits for food, health, home, and relationships. Take home: a first map of your world and its kits.

2. The Power of One

Explore focus, repetition, simplicity, and how Jesus can turn ordinary recurring work into a place of learning. Take home: your household's food priorities.

3. Your One Week Menu

Choose a modest, repeatable set of meals with appropriate variety, household consent, nutrition, cost, and pleasure. Invite Jesus into the conversation and listen to one another. Take home: a seven-day draft menu.

4. Assemble the Food Kit

Gather recipes, ingredients, equipment, storage plans, preparation steps, and shopping lists. Ask what can be grown, shared, borrowed, or traded. Take home: a usable food-kit checklist.

5. Run Your Home Restaurant

Cook, serve, clean, record what worked, and improve the system. Reveal the secrets and the trade-offs. Take home: one tested week and a revision list.

6. Help a Friend; Grow Zion

Bring a Helper Friend. Learn to guide without taking over, to share what you know freely, and to connect neighbors who can help one another. Take home: a plan to teach or accompany one other household.

Before lesson one: the restaurant you've already been running

Matthew 25 · The inheritance of becoming witting

What if Jesus Mountain Restaurant didn't begin with a sign or a website? What if it began decades ago with pancakes, dishes, family dinners, and bearing one another's burdens? In Matthew 25:38–40, feeding another person can be understood as serving Jesus. Our first act in this classroom is not to buy anything. It is to notice what Jesus may already have been growing in our homes.

In Jesus-Greg WORLD, we call this witting: becoming conscious of a treasure we already possessed. The food, the kitchen, the family, the recipes, the neighbors, the memories were already here. Now we ask Jesus to give us eyes to see, and hands to redeem what is here with a new story.

First reflection: Recall one ordinary meal you served or received. Who was fed? What burdens were lightened? What might change if you recognized that scene as part of your own little Zion restaurant?

Six-session working guide: what Jesus and we actually do

Each session includes a Jesus-Greg question, a practical exercise, and a tangible artifact. This is a developing prototype, not a claim that a six-week course has already been tested.

Session 1 · Your World, Your Kits

Jesus encounter: Imagine Jesus walking with you through your home and naming the gifts you overlooked. What if He has been preparing your little Zion restaurant for decades? Jesus question: What have You already put in my hands that I have not recognized?

Do: Sketch a circle labeled My World. Add the recurring activities that make up your life; identify practical kits for Food, Home, Health, Relationships, and other important areas. Mark where the food system touches the others. Ask every participant to name one existing blessing or skill rather than beginning with deficits.

Make: A one-page World → Kits → Food Kit map, with a brief family-food memory.

Session 2 · The Power of One and SOS

Jesus encounter: Imagine Jesus beside you during a repetitive kitchen task, not impatient with the repetition but showing how small and simple things become great. Sufficiency, Order, and Simplicity are ways to make room for love rather than merely optimize labor. Jesus question: What one small and simple practice might You help me redeem?

Do: Use SOS: Sufficiency, Order, Simplicity to examine one recurring meal. Define what is enough; put ingredients, steps, tools, and timing in a useful order; remove needless decisions without sacrificing joy or hospitality. Contrast a scattered routine with a repeatable one.

Make: A one-meal routine card listing what is needed, what happens first, and how to know it worked.

Session 3 · Build Your One Week Menu

Jesus encounter: Read Matthew 25:38–40 and look again at the faces around the table. Jesus is not an extra guest added after the menu is finished: in the story He is encountered through the people being fed. Let their preferences and burdens shape the menu. Jesus question: Who is already at my table, and have I listened to them?

Do: List seven dinner slots; select repeatable dishes from actual household preferences and constraints. Consider nutrition, cost, leftovers, available labor, dietary differences, and celebrations. Invite family discussion and common consent instead of imposing the cook's favorites. Name which ingredients can serve several meals.

Make: A dated seven-day menu with two substitutions and a family feedback question.

Session 4 · Assemble the Food Kit

Jesus encounter: Imagine Jesus showing you that pantry shelves, frozen ingredients, and written recipes are not spiritually empty things. They are provisions for future acts of love. Organizing them is one way to prepare to serve Him in the people who will need feeding tomorrow. Jesus question: How can preparation make it easier to feed the people I love?

Do: For the seven meals, gather recipes, ingredient quantities, pantry/freezer inventory, equipment, storage locations, shopping list, and make-ahead actions. Separate what we have from what we need. Build one shared-ingredient preparation process. Use the original Food Kit sequence: Plan → Gather/Store → One-time setup and routines → Food Story.

Make: A Food Kit binder or folder with four sections, plus a shopping and preparation checklist.

Session 5 · Run Your Home Restaurant

Jesus encounter: Give the meal a scene in Jesus-Greg WORLD: Jesus at the table, the visible family, and the unseen multitude in your sacred imagination. Then pay attention to real people: who is weary, who is carrying too much, who needs welcome? The prototype of Zion is measured in care, not theatrical perfection. Jesus question: Can the work of serving become a scene of Zion rather than a burden nobody sees?

Do: Run one meal from the kit. Record time, cost estimates, quantities, ease of preparation, what guests enjoyed, cleanup, and what to change. Let a second person follow the instructions without the author intervening. Revise any confusing step. Look for a moment of gratitude, fellowship, or service that might otherwise have gone unnoticed.

Make: A tested recipe-and-routine card and a short improvement log.

Session 6 · Helper Friend and Sharing the Harvest

Jesus encounter: Imagine Jesus telling you that the inheritance is not a franchise you must control. You may show one friend how to cook and organize; another person may carry the idea across the earth. Give away the recipe and rejoice. The gift grows when someone else can receive it. Jesus question: What if this treasure is meant to bless someone else, even if I only teach one person?

Do: Invite a friend to choose one manageable food-system goal. The helper asks, listens, demonstrates only when invited, and leaves ownership with the learner. An AI assistant may quietly help draft a menu, convert quantities, or summarize instructions, but should not interrupt or replace the human relationship. Share recipes and methods freely, including failures and trade-offs. Adapt the language for a secular community organization if that helps others use the practical framework.

Make: A one-page handoff guide that another household can actually use, and a plan for one follow-up conversation.

The original Food Kit's unfinished handbook: Jesus redeeming the old work

Recovered work from 2008 already envisioned an A-to-Z handbook: a map of the whole food system with hyperlinks, recurring preparation and maintenance routines, links between food and non-food activities, child teaching, and evaluation tools. The Patio Classroom is a new setting for finishing that old work—redeemed with a story, not invented from nothing.

Our provisional handbook structure is: A. See the existing table; B. Map the world and Food Kit; C. Choose the One Week Menu; D. Inventory and gather; E. Prepare once, serve repeatedly; F. Run and revise; G. Teach a friend; H. Keep the food story. These letters are a draft teaching scaffold, not a transcription of the original workbook.

Prototype principle: This classroom is a small enacted glimpse of Zion, not a prediction that every future restaurant will operate exactly this way. We hope it brings more Jesus, more order, and more coherence into ordinary life now.

The unseen faculty: Jesus is not a decorative instructor

Before each session, name a specific moment when an ordinary act might be recognized anew as service to Jesus. During the session, make something that will actually be used. Afterward, record a witting moment: what had been present all along that we now see differently? Keep the resulting story beside the practical worksheet, so neither the spiritual meaning nor the real work gets lost.

Come as a learner, a teacher, or a Helper Friend. In our Jesus-saturated story, the visible instructor is not the only instructor. We will pray, ponder, ask questions, experiment, and look for the gifts Jesus may be placing in the room. We needn't pretend to know every detail of His unseen work to make room for Him in every detail of ours.
