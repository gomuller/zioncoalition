THE PATIO CLASSROOM · WORKING CURRICULUM

Come to dinner. Come back to learn with Jesus.

You're not coming alone. Pull up a chair. Make room for the angels. We are building this classroom on the conviction that Jesus has been at the table all along—and that there are more with us than we previously supposed.

You like our fruits? Come learn to grow your own tree—with Jesus.

A restaurant that gives away its secrets

Enjoy our food? We will show you the recipe, the technique, the shopping, the preparation, and the compromises we made. We'll help you make it healthier, cheaper, or better suited to your home. We may teach you to grow the herbs—or introduce you to a neighbor who grows them and might trade for your eggs. Our purpose is not to keep you dependent on our kitchen. It is to help you flourish in yours, with Jesus.

We imagine Jesus teaching through the cook, the child with an unexpected question, the gardener, the neighbor, and the friend who accompanies you. Every meal can become an entrance into His larger work.

The Power of One: why a One Week Menu?

We are practicing Getting to One: sufficiency, order, simplicity, and a life increasingly gathered around Jesus. One carefully developed meal can teach dozens of skills. A small, repeatable set of meals becomes a dependable weekly rhythm, while leaving room for discovery, celebration, and variety. One thing becomes many. By small and simple things are great things brought to pass (Alma 37:6).

Six sessions: build your Food Kit

1. Your World, Your Kits

Begin with the end in mind. Imagine your own world, with Jesus at its center and practical kits for food, health, home, and relationships. Take home: a first map of your world and its kits.

2. The Power of One

Explore focus, repetition, simplicity, and how Jesus can turn ordinary recurring work into a place of learning. Take home: your household's food priorities.

3. Your One Week Menu

Choose a modest, repeatable set of meals with appropriate variety, household consent, nutrition, cost, and pleasure. Invite Jesus into the conversation and listen to one another. Take home: a seven-day draft menu.

4. Assemble the Food Kit

Gather recipes, ingredients, equipment, storage plans, preparation steps, and shopping lists. Ask what can be grown, shared, borrowed, or traded. Take home: a usable food-kit checklist.

5. Run Your Home Restaurant

Cook, serve, clean, record what worked, and improve the system. Reveal the secrets and the trade-offs. Take home: one tested week and a revision list.

6. Help a Friend; Grow Zion

Bring a Helper Friend. Learn to guide without taking over, to share what you know freely, and to connect neighbors who can help one another. Take home: a plan to teach or accompany one other household.

The unseen faculty

Come as a learner, a teacher, or a Helper Friend. In our Jesus-saturated story, the visible instructor is not the only instructor. We will pray, ponder, ask questions, experiment, and look for the gifts Jesus may be placing in the room. We needn't pretend to know every detail of His unseen work to make room for Him in every detail of ours.
