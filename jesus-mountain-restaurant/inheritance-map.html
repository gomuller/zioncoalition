Inheritance Map

Things as they were. Things as they are. Things as they may become.

How the ordinary work of feeding people might be seen anew as part of Jesus' story with us.

How to read this map

Already practicedBuilt or being builtImagined / not yet tested

These categories keep our feet on the ground. A practice can be old while the story we tell about it is new. The arrows suggest connections worth exploring; they are not proof of a divine plan. The Jesus-centered interpretation is a testimony and invitation to reflection, not a demand that every learner share the same conclusion.

The picture

A visual working hypothesis, not a history of confirmed divine causes. The map is deliberately open to revision.

Three layers of the inheritance

1 · What was already there

Family meals, pancakes, kitchen repetitions, a weekly menu, storage and preparation systems, and the older Food Kit. These are the practical materials that make the restaurant more than an idea.

2 · What we are constructing

The restaurant website, menu, Patio Classroom, Lesson #1, the Food Story approach, and this public documentary. These are artifacts and prototypes—not proof that a household teaching system works.

3 · What may grow

One real learner, improved lessons, hospitality, freely shared methods, community helpers, and possibly new connections among the hoped-for hundred inheritances. These remain invitations and questions.

Six connections worth noticing

  1. Pancakes → Matthew 25: feeding a family member can be reconsidered as service to Jesus in the person being fed.
  2. One Week Menu → household common consent: planning dinner can become a small practice in listening, negotiation, and love.
  3. Food Room → hospitality: gathering and storing food can support readiness to serve rather than mere accumulation.
  4. Food Kit → Getting to One: sufficiency, order, and simplicity can free attention for people.
  5. HimChi → the backstage story: a familiar food-related object can remind us of the Food Room and the hidden work that makes a shared meal possible.
  6. Patio Classroom → other inheritances: teaching someone to notice existing gifts may become a method for discovering what else has been growing unnoticed.

The sacred question

“Lord, when saw we thee an hungred, and fed thee?” — Matthew 25:37

In the story Greg is exploring, the surprise is not that Jesus suddenly arrives at the restaurant. The surprise is that the people being fed may have been where Jesus invited him to look all along. The food was real before the name was invented. The family care was real before the diagram existed.

Inheritance #1 may therefore be both a restaurant and a way of seeing: the first deliberate exercise in noticing gifts already received, organizing them for loving service, and asking what else might be waiting to be recognized.
