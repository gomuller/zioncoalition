JESUS TV

The Actor · The Audience · The Spirit

The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment — By Saint Greg

Every morning, I begin an all-day opera. The opening scene is simple: I am on my Jesus Pillow, and I begin singing Jesus, the Very Thought of Thee.

But I sacredly imagine something else is happening. I am watching a show. I am watching Jesus TV.

And I am in it.

1. The curtain rises on an ordinary human

The first scene is not spectacular. A man wakes up. He is tired. He has a pillow and a song. He chooses what to feed his head first.

Jesus, the very thought of thee
With sweetness fills my breast;
But sweeter far thy face to see
And in thy presence rest.

There! The opera has begun. The actor sings. And the audience—also me—watches what happens next.

In my sacred imagination, this is not merely a performance for somebody else. It is a way of noticing my own life as it unfolds.

2. We are self-referent creatures

Psychology describes forms of self-awareness and metacognition: we can notice our own thoughts, feelings, and behavior. Sociology offers related perspectives on the self as socially formed and reflexive. We can imaginatively take the perspective of an observer toward our own actions.

That does not make the self a literal crowd. Nor is the sociological idea of collective conscience simply an inner audience: Émile Durkheim used that term for shared beliefs and moral understandings in a society. But these ideas give me useful theatrical language.

I can be an actor on a stage and, in another sense, an audience member watching the actor. I can even become an appreciative or questioning critic of my own performance.

I watch Jesus TV while creating something for me to watch.

Feed your head, the Dormouse said. I choose to feed mine a first thought about Jesus.

3. The show I most want to watch

What if the most interesting thing about this human on the screen is not what he can accomplish by himself?

What if I am watching a person being wrought upon by the Holy Spirit and Jesus?

That is my sacred interpretation, my wager, not a claim that a camera or an outside observer could verify the presence of a Spirit. I believe a human life can be influenced, animated, invited, and transformed by divine companionship.

And sometimes, in my religious language, I call that being possessed by a Spirit. Perhaps multiple spiritual influences are felt at different times. I use this as devotional and metaphorical language for willing receptivity—not as proof of literal external control, and not as an excuse to surrender discernment or responsibility.

I am looking for the fruits: greater charity, clearer judgment, a willingness to serve, and the strength to rest.

4. Inviting the Director

My playful vocabulary includes invite, invoke, conjure. I like the dramatic sound of those words. But I cannot command the Holy Spirit to appear on cue, like an actor waiting in the wings.

What I can do is make room. I can pray. I can remember. I can sing. I can practice noticing. I can respond to what seems good and test those impressions against conscience, scripture, love, and their consequences.

I can prepare the stage without pretending I control the Director.

5. Jesus, I am tired of choosing my own way

Here is the prayer beneath the whole production:

Jesus, I am so tired of choosing my own way. How ’bout You take over? I'll go along with whatever You invite, entice, and persuade me toward. Help me recognize what is good. Help me be willing. Help me rest.

That does not mean I cease making choices. It means I want my choices to become increasingly responsive to a relationship I trust. I want to become less preoccupied with manufacturing my own importance and more willing to receive the next small invitation.

And if I am mistaken about a particular impression? Then I can correct course. The experiment includes humility.

6. An all-day opera with no ordinary ending

The first song is only the opening scene. Later there may be breakfast, a repair job, a conversation, a nap, a memory, a song, an unexpected interruption. Any of them can become another scene in Jesus TV.

The actor lives. The audience notices. The believer sacredly imagines the Spirit working upon him. And the next scene is not yet written.

I am watching a person—me—learning to be moved upon. I am creating something to watch while hoping to discover what Jesus creates in me.

Maybe this is the deepest connection between my Jesus Pillow and Jesus TV: the show begins with a thought, but what I most desire is presence and rest.

The first thought is Jesus. The first scene is my own life. And the opera is what happens next.
