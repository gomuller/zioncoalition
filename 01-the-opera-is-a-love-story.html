The Opera Is a Love Story

Jesus Courting a Human

I sense that my opera with Jesus, Heaven Can’t Wait, is, above all things, a love story.

There are many layers to the opera. There is work. Worship. Scripture. Music. Improvisation. Reenactment. Comedy. Failure. Redemption. Ordinary life. My Jesus-Greg WORLD. My strange little objects and reminders. Songs I've redeemed. Rooms that become scenes. Scenes that become scripture study. And perhaps, eventually, a whole life that becomes an opera.

But underneath all those layers may be something much simpler:

Jesus is trying to win my heart.

More precisely, I sense that this opera is being designed by Jesus—with me helping—to carefully entice, invite and persuade me to love Jesus MORE than before.

That phrase has become increasingly important to me:

Do I love Jesus MORE than before?

Maybe that's the KPI.

Not: Did Greg put on a good show?

Not: Did Greg get all the theology right?

Not: Did Greg sing the songs correctly?

Not even: Did Greg successfully perform his Jesus-Greg Groundhog Day?

But:

Did Greg fall a little more in love with Jesus today?

And the eventual aim is enormous: to love Jesus with all my heart, might, mind and strength.

That changes the way I look at the opera.

Because if this really is a love story, then I should start watching for Jesus' woo moves.

Jesus Is Courting a Human

There is something almost funny about the situation.

Jesus is courting a human being.

Me.

And humans are peculiar creatures.

We don't simply decide intellectually that someone is worthy of love and then instantly love them with everything we've got.

We are affected by experiences.

We notice beauty.

We admire strength.

We respond to kindness.

We remember who stayed with us when things went badly.

We like people who make us laugh.

We become attached to people who know our peculiarities.

Music gets inside us.

Shared adventures matter.

Repeated experiences matter.

Little objects acquire enormous emotional significance.

A glove can mean something.

A pillow can mean something.

A song can mean something.

A room can mean something.

A meal can mean something.

One particular Tuesday morning can mean something forever.

This makes me wonder whether much of what I am experiencing in my Jesus-Greg WORLD isn't random ornamentation around the relationship.

Maybe some of it is how the relationship gets built.

If Jesus wants Greg to love Him with all his heart, might, mind and strength, then Jesus is dealing with the actual machinery of a human heart.

So I want to become a student of that machinery.

What makes humans fall deeply in love?

What makes someone increasingly precious to us?

What makes us trust?

What makes us long?

What makes us remember?

What makes another person gradually move from the periphery of life toward the center?

Here are 100 human pathways into love.

I am not claiming Jesus necessarily uses every one of them. I am creating an inventory so that I can become better at noticing what I experience as His woo moves.

  15. Gentleness — Great strength combined with gentleness is particularly compelling.
  16. Protection — Humans bond with those who make us feel safe.
  17. Rescue — Being rescued creates powerful attachment and gratitude.
  18. Provision — Someone repeatedly meeting our needs becomes associated with goodness.
  19. Reliability — Love grows when someone keeps showing up.
  20. Faithfulness — Someone who refuses to abandon us becomes increasingly lovable.
  21. Sacrifice — We are moved when someone pays a price for our welfare.
  22. Costly love — The greater the apparent cost, the more seriously we take the love.
  23. Vulnerability — Love deepens when another allows themselves to be vulnerable.
  24. Shared suffering — Someone who suffers with us becomes close to us.
  25. Empathy — Feeling that another actually feels with us creates connection.
  26. Comfort — We become attached to whoever repeatedly meets us in sorrow.
  27. Presence — Sometimes simply being there is enough.
  28. Companionship — Humans were not built to flourish entirely alone.
  29. Friendship — Romance-like devotion often grows from friendship.
  30. Constancy — “Still here” can eventually become more persuasive than words.
  31. Delight — We love people who seem genuinely glad we exist.
  32. Affection — Affection tends to awaken affection.
  33. Reciprocity — Humans commonly love those who first love us.
  34. Admiration — Being admired can draw us toward the admirer.
  35. Encouragement — Someone who sees what we might become becomes important to us.
  36. Belief in us — We bond with those who believe in us when we struggle to.
  37. Calling forth our best — Love grows toward someone around whom we become better.
  38. Seeing hidden potential — “You see something in me nobody else sees.”
  39. Giving dignity — Being treated as valuable changes relationships.
  40. Respect — Love flourishes where our agency and personhood are respected.
  41. Freedom — Freely chosen love can feel more meaningful than compelled obedience.
  42. Invitation — Humans often respond better to “come” than “you must.”
  43. Courtship — Gradual pursuit allows anticipation and attachment to develop.
  44. Mystery — We are fascinated by someone we cannot completely comprehend.
  45. Discovery — Love grows as there is continually more to discover.
  46. Surprise — Unexpected goodness gets our attention.
  47. Novelty — New experiences can intensify emotional connection.
  48. Wonder — Awe can open the heart.
  49. Transcendence — Humans hunger for something larger than themselves.
  50. Glory — Magnificence attracts attention and admiration.
  51. Intelligence — Great intelligence can be deeply attractive.
  52. Wisdom — We love someone who repeatedly sees what we couldn't see.
  53. Creativity — Creative brilliance fascinates us.
  54. Humor — Shared laughter creates intimacy.
  55. Playfulness — Love isn't sustained by solemnity alone.
  56. Adventure — Someone who takes us somewhere extraordinary becomes part of the story.
  57. Risk — Shared risk can accelerate bonding.
  58. Challenge — Someone who stretches us can become enormously important.
  59. Teaching — A great teacher changes how we experience reality.
  60. Revelation — Discovering something through another person can bind the discovery to them.
  61. Shared secrets — Intimacy grows around things known between “you and me.”
  62. Private language — Nicknames, symbols, jokes, songs and rituals create a little shared world.
  63. Music — Music can attach emotion to relationship extraordinarily powerfully.
  64. Story — Humans understand ourselves through narrative.
  65. Shared history — “Look what we've been through together.”
  66. Memory — Repeated recollection consolidates attachment.
  67. Ritual — Repeated meaningful encounters deepen bonds.
  68. Symbols — Ordinary objects can become saturated with relational meaning.
  69. Places — Rooms, roads, beds, tables and landscapes can become relationship landmarks.
  70. Anniversaries and milestones — Humans organize love through remembered moments.
  71. Food — Eating together is an ancient bonding mechanism.
  72. Work — Doing meaningful work together produces companionship.
  73. Rest — Feeling safe enough to rest with someone signals trust.
  74. Morning — Beginning the day with someone gives them psychological prominence.
  75. Night — Ending the day with someone does the same.
  76. Repetition — What we repeatedly attend to becomes increasingly central.
  77. Anticipation — Looking forward to the next encounter intensifies desire.
  78. Absence — Missing someone can reveal how attached we've become.

  80. Longing — Desire itself can deepen attachment.
  81. Gratitude — Repeated gratitude can ripen into love.
  82. Dependence — Recognizing how much we receive from another changes our perception of them.
  83. Trust — Entrusting increasingly important things to someone creates intimacy.

  85. Transformation — We love someone associated with becoming more alive.
  86. Healing — The one connected with our healing can occupy a unique place in our heart.
  87. Redemption — Someone who helps turn catastrophe into meaning becomes unforgettable.
  88. Second chances — Humans can love deeply the one who says, “Try again.”
  89. Hope — We gravitate toward someone who makes tomorrow seem possible.
  90. Meaning — Someone who makes our life intelligible becomes extraordinarily important.
  91. Purpose — Shared purpose binds people together.
  92. Mission — “We're doing this together” creates an especially powerful bond.
  93. Belonging — Humans desperately want somewhere—and someone—to whom they belong.
  94. Home — The deepest love can eventually feel like coming home.
  95. Being fully seen without fleeing — Perhaps one of the strongest human experiences of intimacy.

  100. Being loved first — The great Christian courtship proposition: “We love him, because he first loved us.” — 1 John 4:19

Watching for the Woo Moves

Now I can look at my opera differently.

Instead of seeing a bunch of strange pieces that somehow accumulated around my relationship with Jesus, I can ask:

Which human pathway into love is operating here?

A redeemed classic-rock song might be music + private language + memory + repetition.

My Jesus Pillow might be symbol + presence + affection + remembrance.

Putting on my work gloves might become ritual + work + mission + shared language.

My Jesus Shower might involve ritual + repetition + morning + embodied memory.

A mistake during the opera might unexpectedly become patience + forgiveness + second chances + love surviving failure.

Working for somebody might become mission + companionship + purpose.

An improvised lyric might produce surprise + creativity + discovery.

A scripture scene might involve story + teaching + revelation + imagination + shared history.

The Womb/Tomb Scene might become redemption + transformation + hope + rebirth.

And suddenly I can see why an opera might be an especially interesting vehicle for this project.

An opera can carry nearly everything.

Music.

Story.

Repetition.

Beauty.

Drama.

Comedy.

Suffering.

Surprise.

Memory.

Symbols.

Characters.

Costumes.

Rooms.

Objects.

Work.

Rest.

Failure.

Rescue.

Longing.

Reunion.

And love.

The opera can become a container in which thousands of tiny relational experiences accumulate.

The KPI

This also gives me a very different way of evaluating Heaven Can't Wait.

The KPI doesn't have to be production quality.

Some days the singing may be lousy.

Some scenes may flop.

The timing may be wrong.

I may forget lyrics.

The technology may malfunction.

The camera may be pointing at the wrong thing.

Greg may be Greg.

But perhaps none of those things determines whether the opera succeeded.

The deeper question remains:

Do I love Jesus MORE than before?

Maybe by only 0.001 percent.

Fine.

Tomorrow we do another scene.

Another song.

Another meal.

Another scripture.

Another mistake.

Another rescue.

Another laugh.

Another surprise.

Another ordinary object that somehow becomes saturated with meaning.

Another day of shared history.

And then another.

And another.

Being Loved First

Number 100 may finally explain the other 99.

Being loved first.

“We love him, because he first loved us.”

Maybe the ultimate purpose of all these woo moves isn't for Jesus to manipulate Greg into producing an emotion called love.

Maybe it is for Greg to become increasingly capable of noticing love that he believes was already there.

That is quite different.

The songs help me notice.

The scriptures help me notice.

The objects help me notice.

The rooms help me notice.

The opera helps me notice.

My Jesus-Greg WORLD helps me notice.

And perhaps my capacity to perceive the Beloved changes.

That is #99:

Sometimes the beloved hasn't changed. Our eyes have.

So maybe Heaven Can't Wait is partly the story of a human being learning to see.

And the more Greg sees what he understands Jesus to be doing—the patience, humor, invitations, forgiveness, teaching, companionship, surprises, rescues, creativity, mercy, beauty, shared work and persistent presence—the more material Greg has from which love can grow.

Which means the great dramatic question running underneath the entire opera may be extraordinarily simple:

Can Jesus win Greg's whole heart?

Not by force.

Not by command.

Not merely because Greg thinks he ought to love Jesus.

But through encounter after encounter after encounter until Greg increasingly discovers:

I love Him.

And then:

I love Him MORE than before.

And perhaps, someday:

with all my heart, might, mind and strength.
