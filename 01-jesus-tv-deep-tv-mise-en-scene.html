The Divine Art of Mise-en-Scène

How much meaning can Jesus pack into a single scene of your life?

JESUS TV = DEEP TV

DOES JESUS SPEND AN ASTONISHING AMOUNT OF TIME AND ENERGY SETTING UP THE SCENES OF OUR LIVES?

DOES JESUS DO MISE-EN-SCÈNE?

Mise-en-scène is a filmmaking term referring to the arrangement of everything placed before the camera: the setting, lighting, costumes, objects, actors, their movements, and their relationships within the scene.

But great filmmaking goes even deeper.

Sometimes an astonishing amount of intentional meaning is packed into just a few seconds of film.

And what if the same is true of just a few seconds of YOUR LIFE?

Think about what happens when a masterful film scene is constructed.

Writers, directors, cinematographers, composers, editors, and production designers collaborate to create something that operates on multiple levels simultaneously.

Consider these layers:

  • Surface story: What is literally happening? What do we see and hear?
  • Character psychology: What is someone thinking, feeling, remembering, concealing, or discovering?
  • Symbolism: What do objects, colors, gestures, lighting, and locations suggest?
  • Foreshadowing: What seemingly insignificant details acquire extraordinary meaning much later?
  • Musical meaning: How does a melody, lyric, or recurring musical theme connect otherwise separate moments?
  • Intertextuality: How does a scene reference other stories, films, scriptures, myths, songs, or cultural memories?
  • Whole-film architecture: How does one scene echo, reverse, anticipate, or complete another scene elsewhere in the movie?
  • Personal discovery: What meanings does the viewer bring to the scene that even the filmmakers may never have consciously intended?

And here's the astonishing thing:

A single movie scene can contain far more meaning than any individual viewer can consciously process in a single viewing.

Filmmakers may spend weeks constructing something the audience experiences in ninety seconds.

And those ninety seconds may become richer with every subsequent viewing.

Watch it again tomorrow. You see something new.

Watch it ten years later. You discover an entirely different layer of meaning.

And here's another fascinating possibility:

The meaning deliberately placed into a scene is not necessarily the limit of the meaning that can be discovered in it.

Now bring Jesus into the picture.

In filmmaking, we talk about mise-en-scène, symbolism, narrative architecture, and thematic layering.

MORE JESUS. MORE MEANING. MORE REALITY.

ART AS MIMICRY — MIMESIS

There's an ancient concept in aesthetics called MIMESIS, from the Greek word for imitation or representation.

For thousands of years, philosophers and artists have explored the idea that art imitates life, nature, and other works of art.

Plato worried that artistic imitation might take us further away from ultimate reality—a copy of a copy.

Aristotle saw something much more promising.

He recognized imitation as a fundamental human capacity. We learn through imitation. We take pleasure in recognizing likenesses. We discover meaning through the act of representing something.

For centuries, artists studied the masters by imitating their work.

They learned to paint by studying painters.

They learned to compose by studying composers.

They learned to sculpt by studying sculptors.

THE APPRENTICE LEARNS BY IMITATING THE MASTER.

And then Oscar Wilde introduced a fascinating reversal:

LIFE IMITATES ART.

Not merely art imitating life.

But life itself being shaped by the images, stories, ideals, and possibilities that art makes available to us.

Now bring Jesus into the picture.

What if BOTH directions are operating in My Jesus-Greg WORLD?

Jesus creates the scenes of life.

I attempt to recognize and imitate His artistry.

And then, through my own artistic constructions, I begin changing how I actually live.

LIFE → ART → LIFE.

JESUS TV → MY JESUS-GREG WORLD → MORE JESUS TV.

The art becomes part of the life.

And the life becomes part of the art.

And the two begin transforming each other.
