Concept White Paper

Favorite Times

A Free Life-Story & Obituary Preparation Tool
A concept for Funeral Consumers Alliance of Utah

The Idea

What have been your favorite times on Earth?

That simple question could become a remarkably gentle way to help people prepare for death— by first helping them remember their lives.

Funeral Consumers Alliance of Utah already helps people make informed, meaningful and affordable choices about death, funerals and after-death care. Its planning resources encourage people to communicate their wishes before they can no longer speak for themselves, and its Funeral Planning Worksheet specifically asks whether a person has written an obituary.

Before you write your obituary, remember your life.

How It Would Work

Favorite Times would invite a person to begin collecting favorite memories— perhaps 10, 50, 100, or eventually 500.

Not necessarily the most important events. Just moments when the person can look back and say:

“I am glad I was alive for this.”

A childhood afternoon. A friend. A road trip. A wedding. A ridiculous family story. A meal. A teacher. A pet. A difficult experience survived. A beautiful place. A song. A person deeply loved.

Users could gradually add a title, short story, people, date or life period, location, photographs, and perhaps audio or video to each memory.

There is no requirement to reach 500. The remembering is the point. The original 500 Favorite Times on Earth experiment explicitly invites people to adapt the idea however they wish.

From Memories to an Obituary

Over time, those individual memories become something larger: a structured portrait of a human life.

When desired, the collection could help produce useful outputs such as:

My Life Story • Obituary Starter • Funeral/Memorial Program Ideas • Favorite Photos • Important People • Favorite Places • Things I Loved • Stories My Family Should Know • Messages for Family

Eventually, an optional AI assistant could help turn the person's own collected material into a draft obituary or life sketch—with the person remaining in control of what is included.

This complements FCA Utah's existing advance-planning tools rather than replacing them.

Why It Fits Funeral Consumers Alliance

FCA Utah describes itself as a volunteer-run educational organization advocating the right to choose simple, meaningful and affordable after-death arrangements. Its work emphasizes peace of mind, knowledge, consumer choice and tools for discussing end of life while people still can.

National Funeral Consumers Alliance likewise emphasizes consumer choice, educational resources at little or no cost, and people's right to make end-of-life choices consistent with their own religious, philosophical and ethical beliefs.

FREE. No subscription. No paywall. No premium obituary.

VOLUNTARY. Ten memories are worthwhile. Five hundred are worthwhile. There is no finish line.

PERSONAL. Religious, spiritual, secular, funny, serious or unconventional—people tell their own stories.

USEFUL BEFORE AND AFTER DEATH. It enriches and preserves life now while leaving useful material for family later.

A Different Kind of Funeral-Planning Tool

Most funeral planning begins with death:

What do you want done when you die?

Favorite Times begins somewhere else:

Tell me about being alive.

That may make it an unusually inviting doorway into advance planning.

When death eventually comes, instead of a family sitting in grief trying to reconstruct someone's life from memory, much of that person's story may already be waiting for them—in the person's own words.

The Proposed Experiment

Build a simple first version branded for Funeral Consumers Alliance of Utah, offer it free to everyone, test it with real users, and learn.

Start small.

Remember a life.
Preserve its stories.
Make the eventual obituary easier.
And perhaps make life a little richer while we're still here.
PAGE TWO — STRATEGIC FIT & AI

Why This Could Become an FCA Tool

The connection between Favorite Times and the Funeral Consumers Alliance mission is surprisingly direct.

National FCA's strategic direction includes publishing educational content in a variety of media and formats at little or no cost for consumers, strengthening online educational resources, and helping affiliates respond to emerging technologies.

FCA already helps people answer practical end-of-life questions: What kind of funeral do I want? What will it cost? What are my rights? Who should make decisions? Burial or cremation? What should my family know?

Favorite Times adds another category:

What do I want remembered about my life?

That belongs naturally beside advance directives, funeral-planning worksheets, obituary resources and other before-death planning materials.

AI Could Make This Much More Than an “App”

We do not need to begin by deciding that this must be an iPhone or Android app. The simplest and most accessible first version may be a free mobile-friendly web app that works on phones, tablets and computers.

A person opens a link and sees one inviting question:

Tell me about one of your favorite times on Earth.

AI could become a gentle interviewing and organizing assistant. Instead of presenting a long form, it could have a conversation:

Where were you?

Who was there?

About how old were you?

What happened?

Why does that memory still matter to you?

Do you have a photograph that belongs with it?

The user could type—or simply talk. AI could transcribe the person's voice, lightly clean up the story, suggest a title and save it as Favorite Time #1.

Want to remember another one?

What AI Could Eventually Do

REMEMBER — Suggest memory prompts involving childhood, family, school, friends, love, work, travel, holidays, funny moments, music, food, places, accomplishments and ordinary unforgettable days.

INTERVIEW — Turn life review into a friendly conversation rather than a questionnaire.

ORGANIZE — Identify people, places, approximate dates, periods of life and recurring themes.

CONNECT — Notice relationships among memories: “You have mentioned camping with your father in six different stories. Would you like to see those together?”

PRESERVE — Combine text, photographs, scanned documents, audio and video into a personal life archive.

WRITE — Using the person's own material, create drafts of an obituary, life sketch, funeral-program biography, memorial webpage or family-history narrative.

SUMMARIZE — Create something as simple as: “The 20 things I most want my family to know about my life.”

PREPARE — Gently connect users to FCA's practical planning resources when appropriate.

SHARE — Create a private link, printable document, family archive or selected memory collection.

An Important AI Principle

AI should help people tell their stories—not tell their stories for them.

The AI should not invent accomplishments, embellish memories or manufacture an impressive obituary. Its job is to ask, listen, organize and help.

The human supplies the life.
AI helps preserve it.

Privacy should therefore be designed into the project from the beginning. People should know what is being stored, who can see it, how it can be exported and how it can be deleted.

Prototype 1: Start Very Small

The first experiment could be almost ridiculously simple:

  1. Visit a webpage.
  2. Click “Add a Favorite Time.”
  3. Type or speak the memory.
  4. Let AI ask two or three optional follow-up questions.
  5. Add a photograph if desired.
  6. Save the memory.
  7. Repeat whenever another memory comes to mind.

A dashboard might simply say:

You have remembered 37 Favorite Times.

Remember another →

After enough memories have accumulated, another option could appear: “What is my life story beginning to look like?” AI could summarize recurring people, places, themes and periods as another invitation to remember.

From a Utah Experiment to a National Resource?

National FCA is an educational organization with a nationwide network of affiliates. That creates a sensible pathway for experimentation:

Utah experiments → people actually use it → we learn what works → FCA Utah shares the results → National FCA can decide whether other affiliates might benefit.

The underlying technology could eventually be common while individual affiliates supply their own branding and local resources.

That turns the idea from “Greg and his friend made a neat obituary app” into a potentially broader consumer resource: a free way to remember a life before family members someday have to write about that life after it is over.

The Larger Idea

Funeral planning usually begins with the assumption:

Someday I will die.

Favorite Times begins with another equally true assumption:

But first, I lived.

AI gives us a new way to sit beside someone and help them remember that life—one story, one photograph, one voice recording, one favorite time at a time.

When the practical moment eventually arrives to write an obituary, prepare a memorial or tell grandchildren who this person was, the raw material is already there.

Not generated by AI.

Remembered by a human being.

Preserved with AI's help.
