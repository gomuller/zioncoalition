Making the Latent Explicit

The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment

What if much of the extraordinary life we seek is already hidden inside the ordinary life we have?

Not fully developed. Not fully recognized. Not yet brought into view. But there, nevertheless—latent, awaiting discovery, cultivation, and expression.

An acorn contains the potential for an oak tree. A sculptor sees possibilities in a block of marble. A mathematician discovers relationships that were operating long before anyone understood them. In each case, something previously hidden, unrecognized, or unrealized becomes explicit.

I have come to suspect that much of my work in My Jesus-Greg WORLD operates according to this same principle.

I am conducting an experiment I call The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment. Its central proposition is that Jesus Christ may be far more involved in the ordinary workings of human life than my inherited religious habits have trained me to recognize.

And if that proposition is even approximately true, the implications are enormous.

Consider music.

A rock song may have been written without any conscious intention of worshipping Jesus. Nevertheless, it may contain longing, gratitude, devotion, beauty, sacrifice, hope, or the desire for redemption. These ingredients are already present. My experiment asks whether they can be deliberately rearranged, reinterpreted, and redirected toward Christ.

I call this Jesus muddling.

I am not necessarily adding new ingredients. I am attempting to discover and release possibilities already latent in the ingredients I have been given.

Consider my Jesus Mountain Restaurant. People have been preparing meals, gathering around tables, and loving one another through hospitality for thousands of years. I am not inventing any of those things. I am attempting to make their Christ-reminding possibilities more explicit.

Or consider my 500 Favorite Times on Earth project.

Ordinary memories become something more when deliberately revisited through the possibility that Jesus was present and active in experiences I had not previously interpreted religiously. The past does not change. But my relationship to the past can change profoundly.

This is also why I am building an entire imaginative world of galleries, museums, watch rooms, television stations, sacred objects, stories, and religious experiments.

I am constructing devices for making the latent explicit.

These devices invite me to notice, remember, interpret, and sometimes imaginatively amplify possibilities that might otherwise remain invisible.

There is an important distinction here. Recognizing a Christ-centered possibility does not prove that Jesus personally arranged every event or inspired every artist. Sometimes I am discovering an existing relationship. Sometimes I am creating a new one through interpretation. Sometimes I am simply experimenting with a possibility I cannot yet demonstrate.

But even that uncertainty can become fruitful.

Alma 32 invites us to give place for a seed, nourish it, and observe what grows. That is remarkably close to what I am attempting.

Perhaps the Kingdom of God is not only something we await, but something whose possibilities we can begin to recognize and cultivate within the world already surrounding us.

Perhaps becoming more like Jesus involves learning to see more of what He sees.

And perhaps one of the great purposes of religious art is not merely to depict sacred things, but to make the sacred possibilities of ordinary things increasingly visible.

My experiment is to find out.

MORE Jesus. More Reality.

Not necessarily a different world.

Perhaps a more fully discovered one.
