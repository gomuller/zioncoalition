Main address

The main address begins at about 01:22 in the revised script.

00:00 There is a version of the Christian life that most people have been taught and a version that actually appears in the Bible. And when it comes to the Holy Spirit, the gap between those two versions is significant. The version most people have been taught goes something like this. You receive the Holy Spirit when you become a Christian. That's it. He's in you. The transaction is complete.

00:23 And from that point on, the Spirit is available. But it's mostly up to you to try harder, pray more, read more, be more disciplined, and live better, which is why so many Christians are exhausted, and why so many of them feel like they're running on empty most of the time, wondering why the Christian life doesn't feel like what they were told it would be. The version that appears in the Bible is different.

00:48 Not completely different. The Spirit does come at conversion. But what the Bible says about what happens after that is something most people were never taught. And when you see it in the original language, it changes everything about how you approach daily life with God. Here is the sentence that rewrites the whole thing. Ephesians chapter 5, verse 18. Paul says, "Do not get drunk on wine, which leads to debauchery.

01:16 Instead, be filled with the Spirit." Most people read that as a one-time instruction. "Receive the Spirit. Be filled, done." But Paul didn't write it in Greek the way we read it in English, and the gap between what he actually wrote and how we typically understand it is where most of the confusion lives. Let me show you what I mean, and then show you practically and specifically what it looks like to live in daily dependence on the Holy Spirit.

01:45 In Greek, verbs carry more information than they do in English. They tell you not just what the action is, but what kind of action: completed, ongoing, instantaneous, repeated. The verb in Ephesians chapter 5, verse 18, “be filled,” is in the present tense, passive voice, imperative mood. Every word of that matters. Present tense means the action is ongoing, not be filled once, not have been filled.

02:15 The present tense in Greek, especially in a command, carries the meaning of continuous, repeated action. Scholars translate this, keep on being filled, or go on being filled. The most literal rendering would be something like "Be continually being filled." Passive voice means "You are not the one doing the filling." The spirit fills. You receive. You position yourself. But you are not the agent he is. This is not primarily about willpower or spiritual discipline. It is about ongoing receptivity to what God initiates. Imperative mood means it is a command.

02:57 Not a suggestion, not an invitation, not a nice idea. Paul is telling the church at Ephesus and by extension, every Christian that being continuously filled with the Spirit is not optional. It is not reserved for pastors or missionaries. It is the standard operating condition of a believer. So the sentence does not say "Be filled with the Spirit once and carry it forward from there." It says "Keep on being filled with the Spirit." Again and again. Continuously. Daily. This is not a past event you are living off. It is a present reality you are meant to be living in. And once you see that, a very important question follows. If being filled with the Spirit is an ongoing need, and not a one-time event, what does that mean in practice? What actually drains the filling? And how does it get replenished?

03:53 Here is the thing nobody says clearly enough. The filling of the Spirit does not maintain itself at constant capacity without any input from you. Look at what happened in Acts. The disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost. Acts chapter 2, verse 4. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit. Clear? Dramatic. Specific. The sound of a rushing wind fills the house. Tongues of fire appear.

04:25 Three thousand people believe the same day. Now go to Acts chapter 4. Peter and John have been arrested by the Sanhedrin. They are brought before the highest religious court in Israel. High stakes. Maximum pressure. Acts chapter 4, verse 8. Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, Wait. Peter was already filled with the Spirit in Acts chapter 2. Why does Luke note that he was filled with the Spirit again, specifically at this moment, before he spoke, because the filling was renewed for the occasion. A fresh supply for a new need. Then Acts chapter 4, verse 31. After Peter and John return, the whole community prays together, and when they had prayed, the place in which they were gathered together was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and continue to speak the Word of God with boldness. The same people who were filled at Pentecost. Filled again in Acts chapter 4, verse 8. Filled again in Acts chapter 4, verse 31. Multiple fillings of the same people. In the same book, within a short span of time, the filling is not permanent at a fixed level. The early church understood something we have largely lost. The inner cup goes dry. The fuel runs low. The Spirit indwells every believer permanently. The fullness, the active influence, and the overflowing supply require ongoing replenishment.

06:00 Think about it the way Paul actually framed it in Ephesians chapter 5, verse 18. He contrasted being filled with the Spirit to being drunk on wine. People who drink understand something about this contrast. You don't stay drunk. The effect fades. And if you want to stay under the influence, you keep drinking. The point is not to endorse drunkenness. The point is to describe the nature of influence. To be under the influence of the Spirit is not a static state you achieve once. It is a dynamic, active, ongoing condition that requires continuous supply. So the first thing we need to settle is this. Being filled with the Spirit every single day is not a sign that something went wrong the day before. It is not a confession of failure. It is simply the nature of how the filling works. Daily, fresh, renewed.

06:55 Again. Before we talk about how to stay filled, we need to understand what empties the cup. Paul gives us two specific warnings in the New Testament that function as the primary drainage mechanisms. One is in Ephesians chapter 4, verse 30. The other is in First Thessalonians chapter 5, verse 19. Ephesians chapter 4, verse 30. Do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption. Grieve. The word is lypeō in Greek to cause sorrow or pain to someone.

07:28 This is remarkable language. Paul is describing the Holy Spirit as a person who can be caused sorrow, not an impersonal force, not a theological concept, but a person with relational sensitivity, who responds to how we live, what grieves the Spirit. The surrounding context in Ephesians chapter 4 tells us directly. falsehood, unrighteous anger, stealing, corrupting speech, bitterness, wrath, clamour, slander, malice.

08:02 These are relational sins, things that damage relationship with God and with people, and they create a distance, a grieving, between us and the one we're supposed to be moving in step with. Here is the honest implication. You can be a genuine Christian, genuinely indwelt by the Spirit, and still be living in a way that grieves him, that creates relational friction, that reduces his active influence in your daily life.

08:31 Not his presence, but his fullness. First Thessalonians chapter 5, verse 19, adds the second mechanism. Do not quench the Spirit. Quench is fire language. You quench a flame by smothering it. The Spirit, described throughout Scripture as fire, can be quenched, not extinguished permanently, but reduced, suppressed, covered, usually by neglect.

08:59 By the slow accumulation of days where we do not attend to his voice, where we make decision after decision without pausing to ask what he would have us do, where busyness or distraction, or the sheer noise of modern life steadily smothers a flame that needed tending. The combination of these two mechanisms tells us something practical. The filling depletes through two channels, active sin that grieves, and passive neglect that quenches, and both need to be addressed if we are going to live in the ongoing fullness Paul commands.

09:33 So how does the refilling actually happen? Practically. Specifically. Not theoretically. The New Testament gives us a clear picture. And it comes from two sources that most people read separately, but which actually describe the same reality from two angles. The first source is Ephesians chapter 5 itself. Paul doesn't just give the command to be filled in verse 18.

09:57 He immediately describes what the filled life looks like in verses 19 to 21. And those descriptions are not just the results of being filled, they are also the channels through which the filling comes. Speaking to one another in Psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making melody to the Lord with your heart, giving thanks always, and for everything to God the Father, submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ. These are not just what happens when you're filled. They are how the filling stays full. Worship, thanksgiving, community, submission.

10:34 These are the conditions in which the Spirit's fullness is maintained and renewed. The second source is John chapter 15. Jesus, the night before he died, taught the disciples the theology of the vine and the branches. Abide in me, and I in you, as the branch cannot bear fruit by itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in me, abide.

11:01 The Greek word is menō, to remain, to stay, to dwell. Not a one-time connection, not a moment of initial attachment, a sustained, continuous remaining. The branch that is attached to the vine doesn't do anything dramatic to receive the sap. It simply stays attached. The attachment is the work. Everything else flows from it. Paul's language of being filled, and Jesus' language of abiding, are describing the same daily reality from two different angles. One emphasizes the active work of the Spirit filling from above. The other emphasizes the receptive posture of the person remaining in connection. Both are true simultaneously.

11:45 So here, in practical terms, is what daily filling looks like in the New Testament, not as a formula but as a pattern, first. Begin each day with surrender, before strategy. The passive voice in Ephesians chapter 5, verse 18 matters here. You are not the one who fills yourself. The Spirit fills. Your role is not to generate the filling through effort, but to position yourself to receive it. The way a sail positions itself to catch the wind. The wind does what the wind does. Your job is to orient the sail. Practically, this means beginning the day not with your to-do list, not even necessarily with a long prayer, but with a posture, a deliberate acknowledgment that you are not sufficient for the day in front of you and that you are asking the Spirit to move. A sentence is enough. "Holy Spirit, I need you in this day. Fill me. Lead me. I am not running this on my own.” That posture of acknowledged dependence is the open sail. The wind is already blowing. You are simply aligning yourself with it. Second, stay in the Word. This is not optional background information. Paul, in Colossians chapter 3, verse 16, gives a parallel passage to Ephesians chapter 5, verses 18 through 21, where he says, “Let the Word of Christ dwell in you richly.” And the results he describes are almost identical to the results of being filled with the Spirit, teaching one another, singing Psalms. Thanksgiving. The parallelism is intentional. The filling of the Spirit and the dwelling of the Word are connected, not identical but deeply linked.

13:39 The Spirit speaks through Scripture. The person who is consistently in the Word is giving the Spirit material to work with, to illuminate, convict, direct, comfort. The person who is not in the Word is functionally quenching the Spirit's primary channel of communication. Third, walk in step, moment by moment. Galatians chapter 5, verse 16 says, “Walk by the Spirit.” Present tense, continuous action, step by step. Not receive the Spirit and then walk on your own. Keep on walking by the Spirit at every fork in the road, every decision, every conversation that could go well or badly depending on whether you yield to the Spirit's nudge or override it with your own preference. Here is what that looks like in practice. Before you send the sharp email, there is a half-second pause where something in you says, “Wait.” That half-second is the Spirit. Before you make the impulsive decision, something in you says, "Not yet."

14:44 That second is the Spirit. The question is whether you yield in that moment or override it. Walking by the Spirit is learning to recognize those moments and to yield rather than push through. The more you yield, the more you develop the sensitivity to recognize the nudge earlier. The more you override it, the duller that sensitivity becomes, and the more the Spirit is, in practice, quenched. Fourth, Return quickly, when you grieve. This is the one nobody talks about enough. The filling depletes through grieving the Spirit. The way to restore it is not more effort or more spiritual discipline.

15:24 It is the same thing that restores any relationship after you have caused grief to the person you love. Acknowledgement and return. First John chapter 1, verse 9. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. Confess is not a complicated ritual. It is the honest naming of what you did and what it was to the one you did it against. Not a performance of contrition, not a negotiation, not a promise about your future behavior you may or may not keep. Just honest naming.

16:04 I said that. And it was wrong. I acted from pride, and I knew it. I overrode you in that moment, and I chose myself instead. That is enough. The cleansing is his work, not yours. And once the air is cleared, the fullness flows again. I need to say something carefully, because there is a way to take everything I just said and turn it into a burden. Being filled with the Spirit every day is not a performance standard. It is not a measure by which you evaluate whether today was spiritually successful. It is not a new law that replaces the old one. Paul commanded the filling. But the Spirit who does the filling is not a taskmaster monitoring your compliance. He is described in John chapter 14 as the paraclete, the counselor, the one called alongside. He is not standing over you. He is standing beside you. When you wake up tomorrow depleted, and you will, because we all do, the response is not self-recrimination about why you ran dry. The response is simply the open posture again. Fill me. And he does.

17:15 The early church understood this. They didn't agonize over why the Pentecost filling wasn't still carrying them six months later. They prayed. The place shook. They were filled again. And they kept going. This is a rhythm, not a destination. You do not arrive at a permanently full state this side of eternity. You come back to the well. Again and again, that is not failure.

17:41 That is the Christian life. The night before Jesus died, he sat with the disciples and told them something that must have sounded alarming in the moment. It is to your advantage that I go away. For if I do not go away, the helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you. John chapter 16, verse 7, it is to your advantage. More useful for you that I leave than that I stay. The disciples had spent three years in the physical presence of the Son of God. They had seen the miracles from five feet away. They had heard the teaching directly from his mouth. They had watched the transfiguration on the mountain and the feeding of the five thousand in the field and Lazarus walking out of the tomb. Three years of direct, unmediated access to God incarnate. Jesus told them it was better that he leave.

18:35 Because if he stayed, he could only be in one place at a time, in one body, in one location. But if he went, the Spirit would come and the Spirit would not be with them the way Jesus was with them. The Spirit would be in them, in every one of them, at all times, in every place, in every century, in every nation, in every language, in every person who has ever believed since that night. That is what you have access to every morning when you wake up. Not something distant and difficult. Not something reserved for particularly spiritual people or particularly holy moments. The same Spirit who hovered over the waters of creation.

19:19 The same Spirit who was upon Jesus at his baptism. The same Spirit who filled Peter in the courtroom and shook the building where the church was praying. That Spirit is not rationed. He is not withheld. He is not waiting to see if you prove yourself worthy. He is already present, already moving, already available. All that remains is for you to open the sail.

19:43 Keep on being filled. Today, tomorrow, every single day of the life that remains.
