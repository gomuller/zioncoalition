JESUS PILLOW MUD GLASSES

The Brighter Day · The Opening of My Eyes · A New World to See

The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment — By Saint Greg

In the opening scene of my daily Jesus TV opera, I wake on my Jesus Pillow and begin singing Jesus, the Very Thought of Thee. And there is another little thing I do. I softly lean my closed eyes into the pillow.

No actual mud. No elaborate ceremony. Just my face, a pillow, a remembered miracle, and a sacredly imagined invitation.

In my mind, I am commemorating the One who opened blind eyes, who I believe is opening my eyes now, and whom I am asking to open them again.

1. When He puts mud on my eyes

In my redeemed song Christ Now Reign, I sing:

When He puts mud on my eyes I can see the brighter day,
Since Jesus came and saved me I've never been the same.

That image comes from John 9, where Jesus makes clay and anoints a blind man's eyes. The startling thing is that Jesus places something apparently obscuring upon eyes that already cannot see. Then the man washes and returns seeing.

What looks like more darkness can become part of a passage toward sight. The mud is not the destination. It is a catalyst.

2. Why mud? Why this ordinary stuff?

Mud is earth and water: messy, common, unfinished material. It can signify obscurity, the mess of human life, and even the mercy of not having everything disclosed all at once. In the story, Jesus does not discard the material world in order to heal. He works with it.

In my Jesus-Greg WORLD, mud has become a symbol of redemption: the ordinary, even inconvenient stuff of existence becoming a medium for transformation. A pillow. A shower. A song. Bread. Me.

And there is a beautiful paradox: the mud can momentarily cover the eyes while participating in their opening. Perhaps some of my own uncertainty preserves room for trust, choice, and discovery before sight becomes clearer.

3. The pillow becomes the mud

Since I don't keep actual mud in my bed, I make a small, tender substitution. I press my eyes softly into my pillow and let it allude to Jesus placing clay upon the blind man's eyes.

It is a mnemonic act—a living parable. I am not claiming that fabric is sacred clay or that this gesture physically heals my eyes. I am giving my body a way to remember what I am asking of Jesus.

When He puts Jesus Pillow on my eyes, I can see the brighter day.

My pillow becomes my symbolic mud glasses. And because I also call the pillow Jesus-is-my-rest, the gesture carries two petitions at once: Open my eyes. Give me rest.

4. Morpheus, Neo, and the world over our eyes

In The Matrix, Morpheus warns Neo that the world he takes for granted has been placed over his eyes, concealing another reality. The image stays with me.

But I turn the image in a different direction. I sacredly imagine Jesus drawing a new world over my eyes—not a deceptive veil, but a way of seeing that I believe is more truthful, more merciful, more alive to His presence.

I am not asking to exchange one unquestioned illusion for another. I want to remain teachable, correctable, and attentive to what is actually there. My wager is that remembering Jesus can help me notice more of reality, not less.

The old world was pulled over Neo's eyes to hide. I invite Jesus to put His mud over mine so I may learn to see.

5. Building the glasses I want to look through

Here is the peculiar part: I am willingly participating in the making of these symbolic glasses. I sing the song. I move my face into the pillow. I remember John 9. I recall the film. I ask Jesus for eyes to see.

I am building a rest-themed, Jesus-themed instrument of attention, and then I am looking through it. I am an actor in the waking scene, an audience member watching Jesus TV, and a disciple asking to be wrought upon.

The pillow is ordinary. The allusion is deliberate. The interpretation is a sacred wager. And the question is what happens to the life I live after getting out of bed.

6. The brighter day begins here

Before I have solved a problem, repaired anything, made a picture, or written a word, I can enact this little prayer:

Jesus, You opened eyes before. I believe You have been opening mine. Please open them again. Put Your special mud on my eyes. Help me see the brighter day. And teach me to rest in what You show me.

Then I lift my head from the pillow. The opera continues. I will spend the day seeing what I can see.

My first thought is Jesus. My first scene is Jesus TV. And my first pair of glasses is a pillow.
