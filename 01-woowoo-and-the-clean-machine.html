The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment

WooWoo and the Clean Machine

The Wonderful, Almost Too-Good-to-Believe Promise of Jesus

Oh, it is wonderful that I can eat a piece of bread and drink a little water and believe that all my sins have been forgiven.

Really? All of them?

That's what I'm experimenting with believing.

Years ago, I wrote a little one-page book called WooWoo and the Clean Machine. WooWoo is a panda bear who gets dirty playing. Fortunately, he discovers a wonderful machine that makes him clean again.

It's a simple story. Almost childishly simple.

And that's the point.

I wrote it because I wanted to cultivate a belief that had been sitting right in the middle of my religion all my life, yet somehow hadn't fully taken root in my heart: Jesus can make me clean. Completely clean. One hundred percent clean.

Not theoretically clean. Not eventually clean. Not mostly clean.

Clean.

But Greg, It's More Complicated Than That!

Yes. I know.

Elder David A. Bednar has explained that merely partaking of the sacrament does not automatically remit sins. Repentance, covenant renewal, and the sanctifying influence of the Holy Ghost matter enormously.

I honor that teaching. I believe it.

God has technical things about His gospel, and those things matter.

But I also believe that little children can worship God beautifully without understanding those technicalities. King Lamoni began with his understanding of the Great Spirit, and that beginning was sufficient for him to reach toward the greater truth Ammon was teaching.

Jesus Himself invited us to become as little children.

So before I become too sophisticated about precisely how the cleansing works, I want to become childlike enough to believe that it really works.

That's my experiment.

I'm not claiming that my working theory captures every doctrinal distinction. I'm choosing to plant a seed of belief and nourish it, as Alma taught.

And the seed is this:

When I sincerely partake of the sacrament, I will believe that Jesus has made me clean. All the way clean.

Isn't That Dangerous?

I can hear the objection.

Greg, aren't you handing yourself a spiritual get-out-of-jail-free card?

Won't you start sinning willy-nilly, knowing that next Sunday you can simply eat some bread, drink some water, and start over?

It's a fair question.

But it misunderstands what I'm trying to accomplish.

I have spent my life returning to Jesus in earnest. Imperfectly, certainly. Sometimes awkwardly. Sometimes carrying the same weaknesses I carried the week before.

But earnestly.

And I intend to keep returning.

The WooWoo story isn't about a panda who discovers that dirt no longer matters. It's about a panda who discovers the joy of being clean.

After WooWoo gets clean, he wants to stay clean!

And when he gets dirty again, he knows where to return.

That's not a philosophy of carelessness. It's a philosophy of hope.

In fact, my experience has been that believing myself forgiven makes me more interested in living cleanly, not less.

It makes me happier. More hopeful. More grateful. More eager to try again.

Those are some of the fruits I'm watching grow from this little Alma 32 experiment.

The Problem With Remaining Dirty

For much of my life, I attended sacrament meeting, partook of the bread and water, and went home without consciously acknowledging that I had been made completely clean.

I believed in forgiveness, of course.

But had someone stopped me outside the chapel and asked, "Are you now one hundred percent clean before God?" I might have hesitated.

Why?

Perhaps because it sounded presumptuous. Perhaps because I knew too much about my imperfections. Perhaps because I was waiting for a feeling of certainty that never quite arrived.

But what if that hesitation itself was keeping me from enjoying something Jesus was offering?

What if I was so concerned about not claiming too much that I was receiving too little?

I don't have to know everything to believe something.

I can wait for knowledge.

Meanwhile, I can exercise faith.

The Wonderful Little Meal

There's something almost comical about it.

A tiny piece of bread.

A little cup of water.

And the Redeemer of the World.

What an extraordinary contrast between the smallness of the outward ordinance and the enormity of the promised blessing.

Of course, the bread and water don't possess magical powers. Jesus is the Clean Machine's reality. His Atonement makes forgiveness possible. The sacrament brings me back to Him.

But I don't want to spend my Sundays so preoccupied with qualifying that promise that I forget to rejoice in it.

I want to remember Him.

I want to believe Him.

I want to go home clean.

And then I want to live as someone who has been made clean.

Will I get dirty again?

Probably.

Will I return?

Absolutely.

And I hope to return with increasing confidence, gratitude, and love.

Because this is the experiment I'm conducting with Jesus:

I would rather risk believing too much in His cleansing power than spend my life believing too little in it.

WooWoo, my little panda friend, has something to teach me.

The Clean Machine works.

And I'm learning to believe it.
