The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment

Zion: Call and Response

Finding the right people, at the right place, at the right time

The impression

I have long carried an impression that I believe came from Jesus.

The impression is this: You need to be with the right people, at the right place, at the right time, doing the right things.

The difficult part is that I do not believe I am supposed to know beforehand exactly who those people are, where those places will be, or how those meetings are supposed to unfold. Jesus knows. I usually don't.

That realization has led me to one of my persistent questions about building Zion: How desperately do I need to search for the people I am supposed to find?

A city needs many builders

I believe God is preparing many different kinds of Zion builders, each with unique gifts, experiences, temperaments, and callings. Just as a city is not built by carpenters alone, Zion will not be built by one kind of disciple. Architects naturally find architects. Engineers gravitate toward engineers. Electricians find electricians. Farmers find farmers. Teachers find teachers. Artists, organizers, caregivers, writers, programmers, musicians, and builders all have contributions that only they can make.

At the beginning, however, everyone arrives as one large crowd simply saying, “I want to help build Zion.” The necessary teams have not yet formed. The puzzle pieces have not yet found one another.

That requires a belief I am only beginning to plant in the Alma 32 soil of my heart.

Perhaps I do not need to hunt anxiously for my people. Perhaps God has spent years shaping each of us into a particular and recognizable kind of Zion builder. The people I am meant to work with may recognize certain signs—not secret signs, but the ordinary signs of personality, conviction, interests, humor, wounds, projects, gifts, and ways of seeing the world. These become little signals that quietly announce who we are.

I do not need to manipulate how people perceive me or strategically manufacture an appealing version of myself. The only impression management I may need is honest self-revelation: being my comfortable, normal, peculiar, sacred-clown self—unless Jesus specifically directs me otherwise.

A call that can be recognized

The LDS film Escape from Germany illustrates something like this. Missionaries scattered throughout Germany needed a way to locate one another. They sang a familiar Latter-day Saint hymn. To the people around them, it was simply another hymn. But to another missionary, it was immediately recognizable. The song became a signal: one person called, and another person who understood the signal responded.

Perhaps Zion builders find one another in much the same way.

Sociologists have long observed that large groups rarely remain random for long. Through repeated interactions, people gradually sort themselves into smaller communities of shared interests, values, experiences, and purposes. Every conversation, project, testimony, question, and response becomes a kind of signal. Most people pass by without noticing. A few recognize something familiar. Over time, hidden groupings quietly emerge.

Perhaps Jesus often works through that very ordinary process.

The business card in Manti

I think I have actually seen this happen.

Years ago, I was actively looking for people who wanted to build Zion. In hindsight, I was a little naïve. I imagined I could simply announce myself, explain my vision, and the right people would come running.

That is not what happened.

One day I attended an emergency preparedness workshop in Manti, Utah. As I was leaving home, I felt impressed to bring business cards. The problem was that I didn't have any. So I grabbed two sheets of 8½×11 paper, folded them, and put them in my pocket.

At the workshop I asked the organizer if I could briefly introduce myself during the announcements. When my turn came, I said something that probably sounded very strange to almost everyone in the room.

I said, “Jesus gave me a Liahona, and He said I can give you one too. If you're interested, I'd be happy to give you my business card.”

A few people raised their hands. Most simply stared at me, wondering whether I was joking.

The answer was yes. And no. I was simply being myself.

When I walked over to those who had raised their hands, I pulled one of the folded sheets from my pocket, tore off little pieces, wrote my website on each one, and handed them out as my “business cards.” It was tongue-in-cheek, completely serious, and perfectly natural for me.

Most people probably thought, That guy is odd.

One man did not.

When we broke for dinner, a man named Stephen Dean walked directly over to me and began asking questions. That conversation became the beginning of a friendship that has lasted for years.

Stephen later told me, “The moment you ripped up that business card, I knew we had business together.”

As it turns out, Stephen had his own unconventional way of handling business cards. Instead of printing his own, he collected other people's cards, stamped CANCELED across the front, and stamped his own information on the back. He was, in effect, recycling business cards. It was quirky, resourceful, and completely unlike the conventional approach.

That was only the beginning.

Over the years I discovered that Stephen and I shared a remarkably similar way of seeing the world. We were both unconventional. We both approached problems creatively. We both believed Jesus often works outside ordinary expectations. Looking back, I don't think either of us consciously recognized the other because of a ripped piece of paper.

I think we recognized one another because we were sending the same kind of signal.

The billion-piece puzzle

Today I wonder if that is often how Jesus gathers His people. We don't always recognize one another because we belong to the same organization or attend the same meetings. Sometimes we recognize one another because, almost without realizing it, we reveal the particular way Jesus has been shaping us. The call is authentic. The response is authentic. Only afterward do we realize that Jesus had quietly orchestrated the meeting all along.

Maybe that is one way Zion will be gathered—not by everyone trying to become the same, but by each disciple becoming more fully the person Jesus is shaping them to be. Like pieces of a billion-piece puzzle, each piece bears its own distinctive shape. We do not force the fit. We simply allow the Master Builder to place us where we belong.

So perhaps the instruction is simpler than I imagined.

Trust Jesus. Keep moving. Become the person He is making you. Sound the call He has given you. Then let Him bring the right people to hear it—and let Him lead you to theirs.

The next call

So my plan is simple. I will join communities devoted to building Zion and offer a few authentic “business card” posts—little signals that honestly reveal who Jesus is shaping me to become. If a moderator declines the post, that is useful information. If the post is approved but receives no response, that is useful information too. And if someone recognizes the call and responds, I will receive that as a gift. In every case, I can trust that Jesus is quietly guiding me toward the right people, at the right place, at the right time.
