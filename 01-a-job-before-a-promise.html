WORKING CONCEPT • NOVEMBER 14, 2026 • FOR OLIVER AND POTENTIAL BUSINESS PARTNERS

A Job Before a Promise

Begin with the end in mind: build and verify a real, flexible, income-producing remote position before asking a vulnerable person to consider taking it.

Important: This is a proposed future outcome, not an existing job offer. The business, its financial performance, and its three-month track record have not yet been established. Kent has not been contacted about or endorsed this proposal, and no funding or participation is requested of him.

The destination: a future email from Greg to Kent

ILLUSTRATIVE DRAFT — TO BE SENT ONLY IF THE CONDITIONS DESCRIBED HAVE ACTUALLY BEEN MET

Subject: A possible work opportunity for Joe

Kent,

You've been helping Joe and his daughter for years, and you've included us in a small part of that journey. They've remained in our thoughts and prayers. My friend Oliver Parler has also continued thinking about whether there might be a practical way to help Joe find reliable work.

Oliver has wanted to meet with you, but I felt the right first step was a note from me—one that asks nothing of you financially and places no new project on your shoulders.

We've been working toward something specific: a flexible, remote position that Joe could step into if he wishes. The work has now been performed successfully by someone else for more than three months, with actual earnings and a documented process. That person is moving into another role, so this is an established position rather than an untested business Joe would have to create.

Here is the link to the position, its duties, schedule flexibility, earnings record, and onboarding process: [insert verified position link when ready].

Because you know Joe and his circumstances far better than we do, I wanted to put this in your hands first. You can decide whether, when, and how to share it. Joe is entirely free to explore it or decline it.

There's no request for money or management from you. We simply hope to make a genuine opportunity available.

With appreciation,
Greg Muller

What Oliver and his partners would build

Develop an actual business-funded remote position; assign someone to do the work first; document the workflow, support needs, net earnings and hours; operate it successfully for at least three months; establish a funded transition into the role; then prepare a simple, voluntary invitation for Joe—or another person facing housing instability.

The investors, not Kent or Joe, bear the startup and testing risk. The existing worker must have a genuine next position before any transition is proposed.

Conditions before Greg contacts Kent

Verified earnings and costs; clear compensation and payment terms; realistic hours that can scale up or down; training and supervision arrangements; necessary equipment and connectivity; an honest assessment of reliability and risks; a documented transition plan; and an actual opening that does not require Joe to invest money or start a business.

Greg alone decides whether and when to approach Kent. Kent remains the trusted intermediary for Joe. Neither Kent's support nor Joe's acceptance is presumed.

What Oliver can say at today's investor meeting

“Our goal is to build a real remote job before we offer it to anyone. We have a possible beneficiary in mind, but no commitment from him or his longtime helper. We will finance, operate, and test the role ourselves, with at least three months of demonstrated results. Only then will Greg decide whether to send the opportunity through the trusted person who knows this family's situation.”

This page describes a proposed model, not a solicitation on behalf of Kent, Joe, or his daughter.
