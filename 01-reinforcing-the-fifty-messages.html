The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment

The Practices Become the Preaching

Why the Fifty Foundational Messages must be reinforced throughout my Jesus-Greg WORLD and Jesus Groundhog Day.

Return to the Fifty Messages

A world teaches what it values

The fifty messages are not merely things I want tomorrow's Greg to remember. They are things I want tomorrow's Greg to live inside of.

Imagine a Babylonian message: “Greg, money is very important. Go get money.” A whole society can reinforce that message without repeating it aloud. Daddy goes to work. Why? To earn money. Children learn to arrive on time, prepare for careers, and measure security in financial terms. The repeated actions reveal what the world values.

The practices become the preaching.

My Jesus-Greg WORLD is an intentional alternative—not a rejection of work, money, or practical security, but a decision to place them within a different story. Instead of merely saying “Jesus is my best friend,” I want to build a world in which friendship with Jesus is enacted, remembered, sung, alluded to, and experienced again and again.

Every message needs its reinforcements

Each of the fifty messages will have two complementary parts:

  1. The Message: What I want tomorrow's Greg to know, remember, believe, practice, and become.
  2. The Reinforcements: A running enumeration of the ways Jesus-Greg WORLD teaches that message through my daily life.

These reinforcements may be direct or indirect: scenes, songs, objects, gestures, scriptures, conversations, work, service, relationships, memory-palace exhibits, and recurring habits. Some explicitly speak the message. Others hint at it, allude to it, or bring it quietly to mind.

One practice may reinforce several messages at once. The more the practices interweave, the more the whole world becomes a living reminder rather than a loose collection of slogans.

Example: Message #4 — Remembering the Multitude

“Those who are with us are more than we previously supposed (and those those are a multitude).” My adapted reminder from 2 Kings 6:16 invites me to notice the mortals and possible immortals who have borne my burdens. I am not alone; I have received goodness through many hands.

My 500 Favorite Times on Earth are more than a record of happy occasions. They are 500 invitations to ask: Where did I know the goodness of God? Who made this moment possible? Who was beside me? Whose sacrifices or unseen work contributed? Whom have I neglected to thank?

Reinforcements already available

  1. The 500 Memories project: Revisit good times, identify the people associated with them, and recognize the help I received. The memories themselves are already part of Jesus-Greg WORLD; the systematic gratitude practice is still being developed.
  2. Latter-day Saint temple work: Baptism for the dead and other ordinances performed on behalf of the dead provide a distinctive, embodied pattern of remembering and serving people who are not visibly present. In temple worship, I participate in a relationship across generations, rather than merely recalling names from the past.
  3. Family photographs and albums: Faces make heritage visible. They remind me that I belong to a family, a community, a history, and a multitude of contributors.
  4. Birthdays, anniversaries, and memorial days: Remembering a parent, grandparent, friend, or benefactor on a recurring date is a ritual of recognition. Like Veterans Day, it says: Your contribution has not been forgotten.
  5. Scriptural remembrance: Mosiah 18:8–9 invites burden-bearing; 2 Kings 6:16 invites me to reconsider the number of those with us. The scriptures provide words to rehearse the message.

Reinforcements I want to develop

  1. One memory, one benefactor: Return regularly, perhaps daily, to one of the 500 memories. Name someone whose goodness helped make it possible; give thanks to God and consider whether to contact that person.
  2. Outreach as gratitude: My former Texas boss Craig Blakely helped my career and graduate-school journey and had my back. I reached out recently. I want to continue reaching out, perhaps monthly or every other month, not merely when I need something, but to honor what he gave me.
  3. Remembering even without contact: Craig may be far away, and I may never see him again in person. Yet I can remember him, see his photograph, tell his story, thank God for him, and sometimes speak with him by telephone. Being unseen does not erase someone's place in my life.
  4. A living register of benefactors: Connect names, photographs, memories, birthdays, gratitude, and possible outreach to my 500 Memories and memory palace.
  5. Daily recognition of present burden-bearers: Notice who is helping me today, especially those whose labor is easy to overlook. And ask whose burdens I can bear in return.

Reinforcing the unseen dimension

Remembering absent mortals suggests a further question: Can I develop comparable rituals of recognition for those I believe may accompany or minister to me from the spirit world? Temple ordinances already give me one embodied pattern for acknowledging relationships across the veil.

I can pray in gratitude for ancestors and possible ministering angels, contemplate their lives, remember their names, or imaginatively address them. But I want to distinguish what I know from what I sacredly guess. I can speak to an absent mortal by telephone and hear a reply. I may speak toward unseen spirits in a different, faith-shaped sense, without claiming that I can verify who hears or that an ordinary reply has occurred.

The ritual does not have to prove the invisible to reinforce my conviction that I am not alone.

In the end, the sequence is remember, recognize, honor, and reciprocate. The 500 Memories are evidence of goodness experienced, invitations to discover its many contributors, and opportunities to become a burden-bearer myself.

A running inventory, not a finished checklist

For each message, I want to enumerate the principal ways it is reinforced throughout Jesus Groundhog Day. Each entry can link to its scene, song, museum exhibit, thought piece, or practical routine. The inventory should distinguish what I already practice, what I am developing, and what I am only imagining.

As I discover more connections, I can add them. As a practice becomes unhelpful, I can change it. The purpose is not mechanical repetition, but an increasingly coherent life in which my most important messages are made present by the things I actually do.

The fifty messages become design specifications for Jesus-Greg WORLD. Jesus Groundhog Day becomes their daily performance.

The messages tell me what matters. My WORLD gives them places to live. And tomorrow's Greg inherits not only the words, but a daily world that keeps speaking them.
