The MORE Jesus = More Reality Experiment

The Practices Become the Preaching

Why the Fifty Foundational Messages must be reinforced throughout my Jesus-Greg WORLD and Jesus Groundhog Day.

Return to the Fifty Messages

A world teaches what it values

The fifty messages are not merely things I want tomorrow's Greg to remember. They are things I want tomorrow's Greg to live inside of.

Imagine a Babylonian message: “Greg, money is very important. Go get money.” A whole society can reinforce that message without repeating it aloud. Daddy goes to work. Why? To earn money. Children learn to arrive on time, prepare for careers, and measure security in financial terms. The repeated actions reveal what the world values.

The practices become the preaching.

My Jesus-Greg WORLD is an intentional alternative—not a rejection of work, money, or practical security, but a decision to place them within a different story. Instead of merely saying “Jesus is my best friend,” I want to build a world in which friendship with Jesus is enacted, remembered, sung, alluded to, and experienced again and again.

Every message needs its reinforcements

Each of the fifty messages will have two complementary parts:

  1. The Message: What I want tomorrow's Greg to know, remember, believe, practice, and become.
  2. The Reinforcements: A running enumeration of the ways Jesus-Greg WORLD teaches that message through my daily life.

These reinforcements may be direct or indirect: scenes, songs, objects, gestures, scriptures, conversations, work, service, relationships, memory-palace exhibits, and recurring habits. Some explicitly speak the message. Others hint at it, allude to it, or bring it quietly to mind.

One practice may reinforce several messages at once. The more the practices interweave, the more the whole world becomes a living reminder rather than a loose collection of slogans.

Example: Message #2 — Prayer

“Talk with Heavenly Father and Jesus. Pray always.” How does tomorrow's Greg repeatedly encounter that invitation?

  1. Waking: My first words, “Jesus, the very thought of thee,” begin the day by addressing Jesus.
  2. The Jesus Pillow: An ordinary object recalls His rest and His power to open my eyes.
  3. Morning hymn: Singing becomes prayer, not merely musical performance.
  4. Clasped hands: A gesture recalls the companionship of Jesus and the Holy Ghost in prayer.
  5. Throughout the day: More scenes, reminders, and practices can be discovered, tried, and added.

“Ere you left your room this morning, did you think to pray?” can become a sung reminder. Or perhaps the prayer itself, already built into the waking scene, does the work. The point is not to accumulate rituals for their own sake; it is to create living reinforcement.

A running inventory, not a finished checklist

For each message, I want to enumerate the principal ways it is reinforced throughout Jesus Groundhog Day. Each entry can link to its scene, song, museum exhibit, thought piece, or practical routine. The inventory should distinguish what I already practice, what I am developing, and what I am only imagining.

As I discover more connections, I can add them. As a practice becomes unhelpful, I can change it. The purpose is not mechanical repetition, but an increasingly coherent life in which my most important messages are made present by the things I actually do.

The fifty messages become design specifications for Jesus-Greg WORLD. Jesus Groundhog Day becomes their daily performance.

The messages tell me what matters. My WORLD gives them places to live. And tomorrow's Greg inherits not only the words, but a daily world that keeps speaking them.
